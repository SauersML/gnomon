FIVE BREAKTHROUGH-SCALE PROBLEMS IN LATENT-STRUCTURE MATHEMATICS
================================================================

Purpose and admission standard
------------------------------

This is a list of problems to attempt, not a claim that any problem is solved or
nearly solved. The problems are stated without reference to any scientific
domain. Each is chosen because its solution would change what can be inferred
from large heterogeneous systems with hidden dependence.

A result belongs on this list only if it gives an existence theorem, a
classification theorem, a sharp universality theorem, or a fundamental
impossibility theorem. A new estimator, a sharper rate, a deterministic
equivalent, a phase diagram for one parametric model, or a repackaging of a known
identity does not count.

The exact formulations below survived a targeted novelty screen against adjacent
work on marginal polytopes, classical extension theorems, product-space
invariance principles, latent-variable identifiability, nonnegative
factorization, graphical identification, and operator pencils. This is not a
logical proof that no equivalent formulation exists anywhere; a dedicated
expert literature audit is required before claiming definitive novelty.

Hard non-overlap rule
---------------------

The following subjects from the previous frontier list are deliberately excluded,
including disguised reformulations:

  * spike detection, weak recovery, rank selection, or critical spectral windows;
  * unknown-background adaptation, whitening, or signal-versus-noise imitation;
  * sparse computational-statistical gaps;
  * uniform projection or correction for later-chosen directions;
  * deterministic equivalents, anisotropic resolvents, interpolation, or ridge;
  * partition kernels, Toeplitz hard edges, or spectral realizability;
  * absorbing-boundary limit experiments;
  * post-selection amplification expansions;
  * residual quadratic-functional confidence bounds; and
  * sketching or compression for later certification.


Problem 1. A local-to-global theorem for overlapping probability laws
=====================================================================

For every integer d, let C_d be a finite cover of {1,...,d} by subsets whose
sizes may grow with d. A local law system is a family

    L_d = {mu_A : A in C_d},

where mu_A is a probability law on R^A and the laws agree exactly on every
overlap. Assume uniform moment bounds and invariance under the automorphism group
of the cover. Call L_d globally realizable if there is a probability law nu_d on
R^d having all the prescribed marginals. Call a sequence asymptotically
realizable if there are global laws whose average marginal discrepancy from L_d
tends to zero in total variation.

The problem
-----------

Find a necessary-and-sufficient local-to-global criterion for asymptotic
realizability for a maximal nontrivial class of growing covers. The criterion
must depend only on intrinsic compatibility data visible in bounded unions of
members of C_d.

A decisive solution must establish one of the following outcomes.

  A. Prove a finite-obstruction theorem: there is a universal radius r and an
     explicit set of inequalities on unions of at most r cover elements whose
     satisfaction is equivalent to asymptotic global realizability.

  B. Construct an explicit coherent sequence L_d which passes every test on
     every bounded union, at every fixed radius, but remains a positive distance
     from every globally realizable system. Prove that no recursively enumerable
     hierarchy of bounded-locality certificates characterizes realizability for
     the stated class.

The result must survive smooth positive densities, uniformly bounded moments,
and highly regular overlap geometry. A finite-alphabet marginal-polytope
example, a parity trick on one cycle, or an ordinary compactness argument is
insufficient.

Why this is breakthrough-scale
------------------------------

The question asks whether perfect agreement of every feasible local view can
still conceal a global incompatibility that no bounded audit can expose. Either
answer would create a general theory of when separately observed pieces of a
large system can be treated as marginals of one coherent law.


Problem 2. Rigidity or wildness of hidden positive-operator cones
================================================================

Let H be a separable Hilbert space. A hidden positive-operator model is a pair
(T,A), where T:H->H is bounded, injective, and has dense range, and A is a weakly
closed convex cone of positive trace-class operators containing a strictly
positive element. Its observable cone is

    O(T,A) = closure{ T D T* : D in A }.

Two hidden models are equivalent if they differ by an invertible change of
hidden coordinates:

    (T,A) ~ (T S^{-1}, S A S*)

for some bounded invertible S. Restrict attention first to cones generated by
smooth finite-parameter sets whose linear span is weak-operator dense in a
commutative von Neumann algebra.

The problem
-----------

Classify the fibers of the map

    (T,A) / ~  ->  O(T,A).

Determine whether equality of the entire observable cone forces equivalence of
the hidden models under natural irreducibility and regularity hypotheses. If
rigidity fails, construct two inequivalent models with identical observable
cones and classify the full ambiguity.

A decisive solution must establish one of these global alternatives.

  A. Rigidity: give intrinsic necessary-and-sufficient conditions on an
     observable cone that guarantee a unique hidden realization, together with
     a reconstruction theorem stable under perturbation.

  B. Wildness: prove that classification of the fibers contains, by an explicit
     Borel reduction, the classification of representations of every finitely
     presented algebra, or another accepted wild classification problem.

Finite simultaneous diagonalization, ordinary matrix-pencil identifiability,
and uniqueness under a known cone do not solve the problem.

Why this is breakthrough-scale
------------------------------

This asks whether a complete continuum of second-order observations determines
the hidden coordinate system that generated it. Rigidity would justify a broad
class of latent decompositions; wildness would prove that no catalogue of
spectral or moment invariants can ever be complete.


Problem 3. An if-and-only-if universality theorem for dependent polynomial chaos
===============================================================================

Let mu_d be a centered probability law on R^d with covariance Sigma_d, uniformly
bounded moments of every fixed order, and no assumption of coordinate
independence. For a multilinear polynomial f_d, define its influence vector by
the L2(mu_d) norms of its coordinate derivatives. Let G_d be Gaussian with the
same covariance Sigma_d.

Say that (mu_d) has covariance universality if, for every sequence of multilinear
polynomials f_d satisfying

    Var_mu(f_d) = 1,
    max_i Influence_i(f_d) -> 0,
    degree(f_d) = o(d),

the distance between the laws of f_d(X_d) and f_d(G_d) tends to zero whenever
the Gaussian comparison sequence has a limit.

The problem
-----------

Characterize covariance universality by necessary and sufficient intrinsic
conditions on the joint cumulant tensors of mu_d.

A decisive result must:

  1. identify an exact condition, not merely a sufficient mixing inequality;
  2. allow the polynomial degree to diverge;
  3. cover measures with long-range, multiscale dependence;
  4. prove universality uniformly over every admissible low-influence
     polynomial; and
  5. construct explicit counterexamples on the opposite side of the boundary.

The strongest version asks whether vanishing of a precise set of normalized
cumulant contractions is equivalent to covariance universality. If no such
analytic characterization exists, prove that no criterion involving cumulants
up to any fixed order can decide the property.

Product measures, fixed-degree chaos, Gaussian inputs, and a central limit
theorem for one chosen polynomial are explicitly excluded.

Why this is breakthrough-scale
------------------------------

The theorem would decide when all sufficiently diffuse nonlinear aggregates
forget everything about a high-dimensional law except its covariance, and when
hidden higher-order dependence survives every low-influence condition. This is
a universality classification, not another normal approximation.


Problem 4. Canonical factorization of a continuum of stochastic kernels
=======================================================================

Let X and Y be compact smooth manifolds and let T be a connected parameter
manifold. Suppose one observes a smooth set of strictly positive stochastic
kernels

    P_t(dy | x),    t in T.

A mechanism-mixture factorization of latent dimension r is a compact
r-dimensional manifold U, a smooth kernel K(dy | x,u), and smooth strictly
positive densities rho_t on U such that

    P_t(dy | x) = integral_U K(dy | x,u) rho_t(u) du

for every (t,x). Two factorizations are equivalent only when related by a smooth
diffeomorphism of U preserving the factorization.

The problem
-----------

Develop a complete theory of minimal mechanism-mixture factorizations.

A decisive solution must:

  1. give intrinsic necessary-and-sufficient conditions for existence at each
     finite latent dimension r;
  2. prove whether the minimum r is identifiable from the observed kernels;
  3. classify all minimal factorizations up to the stated equivalence;
  4. identify the exact exceptional sets for which uniqueness fails; and
  5. prove a perturbation theorem showing when the factorization is stable.

The problem is not ordinary finite nonnegative matrix factorization. The
continuum of x and t, smoothness, positivity, and equivalence under latent
diffeomorphism are essential. Borel encodings that collapse every latent space
to one dimension are forbidden by the smooth category.

An acceptable negative resolution would prove that even real-analytic positive
kernel sets admit inequivalent minimal factorizations of the same dimension and
that their classification is wild in a precise descriptive-set-theoretic sense.

Why this is breakthrough-scale
------------------------------

This asks whether variation across a continuum of contexts can canonically
separate a stable mechanism from a changing hidden distribution. It is a
nonlinear representation theorem with an exact uniqueness question, not a
parametric identifiability calculation.


Problem 5. Complete identification of local and propagated effects on random networks
=====================================================================================

Let (G,o) be an ergodic unimodular random rooted directed network of bounded
expected degree. Each vertex v carries observed marks (X_v,Y_v) generated by a
stationary structural system

    Y_v = theta X_v + F(rooted marked in-neighborhood of v excluding Y_v, xi_v),

where the noises xi_v are conditionally independent given G, F is unknown but
belongs to a fixed regularity class, and the simultaneous equations have a
unique stationary solution. Only the joint law of the marked rooted network is
observed. The scalar theta is the local effect; the term involving other
vertices produces propagated effects.

The problem
-----------

Give necessary and sufficient conditions for theta to be identified from the
observational rooted-network law, uniformly over F and over the admissible law
of G.

A decisive solution must establish a complete dichotomy.

  A. In the identifiable regime, construct an intrinsic functional of the
     observed law equal to theta and prove uniqueness over the entire model
     class.

  B. Outside that regime, construct observationally identical stationary
     systems with different theta, and prove that every failure of the proposed
     criterion generates such an equivalence.

Then determine the weakest additional experiment, chosen from a mathematically
specified class of local perturbations, that makes theta identifiable whenever
identification is possible in principle.

Finite acyclic graphs with known linear F, ordinary instrumental-variable
conditions, and existing graphical criteria for a fixed finite causal diagram
are excluded. The theorem must handle cycles, infinite-volume stationarity,
unknown nonlinear propagation, and random network geometry simultaneously.

Why this is breakthrough-scale
------------------------------

The problem asks for a complete boundary between a genuinely local effect and
an observationally indistinguishable effect propagated through a random
relational system. A solution would be a representation-and-identification
theorem for infinite interacting systems.


Priority and coherence
======================

Problems 1 and 2 are the most foundational. Problem 1 asks whether local
compatibility has a complete route to global coherence. Problem 2 asks whether
complete second-order variation determines its hidden coordinates or whether
the classification is intrinsically wild.

Problem 3 is the strongest universality question: it determines whether
higher-order dependence can disappear from every diffuse nonlinear aggregate.
Problem 4 is the nonlinear latent representation problem. Problem 5 is the
identification problem once the underlying system is relational rather than a
collection of isolated coordinates.

These are deliberately not arranged as a pipeline. Each can fail in a way that
produces a major theorem: a non-approximable coherent local object, a wild
operator-cone fiber, a covariance-matched nonuniversal chaos, inequivalent
analytic minimal factorizations, or two distinct infinite mechanisms with the
same complete observational law.

Final quality test
==================

Before promoting any item as a formal conjecture, its exact statement should
undergo a dedicated expert literature audit. If an existing theorem already
answers the stated quantifiers, delete the item; do not weaken it into a minor
corollary. If an easy counterexample defeats the formulation, strengthen the
hypotheses until the counterexample is excluded without making the conclusion
tautological. The intended output of each problem is a new structural theory,
not an incremental bound.
