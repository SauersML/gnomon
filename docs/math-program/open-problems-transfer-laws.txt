TRANSFER LAWS FOR PARAMETERIZED ATOMIC ENSEMBLES
A program of open problems

Standing instruction to the reader: nothing below is a request for a bound, a
sufficient condition, or an asymptotic without a rate. Every problem asks for a
CHARACTERIZATION -- an if-and-only-if, an exact law with its equality cases, or
a proof that no such law exists together with the exact obstruction. A theorem
that says "at most" is, for this program, a failed attempt at a theorem that
says "exactly".


================================================================================
0. THE SETTING
================================================================================

Fix an integer d >= 2 and a parameter interval T (an open subinterval of R).

A BUNDLE FAMILY is a map t |-> nu_t from T into probability laws on R such that
each nu_t is supported on exactly d points, has mean zero and variance one, and
the atom locations a_1(t) < ... < a_d(t) and masses p_1(t), ..., p_d(t) are real
analytic in t. The name records the essential structure: the d atoms are NOT
independently adjustable. They arrive as an indivisible bundle indexed by a
single scalar, and every constraint on the family is a constraint on a curve in
the (2d-2)-dimensional simplex of centered unit-variance d-atom laws, not on the
simplex itself.

The motivating example, and the one whose rigidity is already settled, is the
BINOMIAL-2 FAMILY: d = 3, masses ((1-t)^2, 2t(1-t), t^2), atoms the standardized
values of {0, 1, 2}. It should not be treated as the general case. Part of the
point of what follows is to discover which of its properties are accidents of
d = 3 and which are structural.

A PANEL is a pair (mu, beta) where mu is a probability measure on T and beta is a
measurable weight function on T, square-integrable against mu. A panel generates,
for each n, an ensemble of n independent coordinates X_1, ..., X_n with
t_i drawn i.i.d. from mu and X_i | t_i ~ nu_{t_i}, together with the READOUT

    R_n = c_n * sum_i beta(t_i) X_i,     c_n chosen so Var R_n = 1.

More generally an ADMISSIBLE DESIGN is a sequence of multilinear forms in the
coordinates with unit variance, diverging minimum degree, and vanishing maximal
influence; the linear readout is the degree-one case and should never be the
only case considered.

A TRANSFER is an ordered pair of panels (P, P') = ((mu, beta), (mu', beta)) --
the same weight function read against two different parameter measures. A
TARGET is a jointly distributed real variable Y coupled to the coordinates. An
ACCURACY FUNCTIONAL A assigns to the joint law of (R, Y) a real number. The
DEGRADATION is

    D(P -> P'; A, design) = A(P') - A(P)   or its ratio, as the functional
                                            dictates.

Every problem below concerns the map from (P, P') to the degradation, and the
overriding demand is that answers be stated in terms of functionals COMPUTABLE
FROM THE INPUTS -- that is, from mu, mu', beta and the bundle family -- and
delivering guarantees about OUTPUTS, the numbers an analyst actually reports.
A characterization phrased in terms of quantities that can only be evaluated
once the answer is known is not a characterization.

Write U = X^2 - 1 for the centered square of a coordinate, and call the law of U
the SECOND FLOOR. Iterating the normalized centered square generates the TOWER;
the modulus laws of its floors, together with their two-jets and arithmetic
types, generate the LADDER. The ladder is known to be reachable by admissible
designs. The odd parts of the floors are known, in one regime, not to be.


================================================================================
1. THE REACHABLE ALGEBRA
================================================================================

PROBLEM 1. Determine exactly which functionals of a panel survive to the limit
of admissible designs.

Precisely: let L be the set of functionals F of (mu, beta) such that for every
admissible design sequence, the limit law of the design depends on the panel
only through F, and let A(L) be the algebra they generate. Characterize A(L).

What a complete answer contains:
  (a) an explicit countable generating ladder for A(L), with the generators
      given as integrals against mu of explicit analytic functions of t;
  (b) a proof of MINIMALITY -- for each generator, an admissible design and two
      panels agreeing on all other generators and separated by that design;
  (c) the exact finite truncations: for each degree k, the sub-algebra reachable
      by designs of degree <= k, and a proof that the filtration is strict;
  (d) an analytic formula expressing an arbitrary limit as a function of ladder
      coordinates, with domain of convergence.

Why this is hard. The reachability question is not a moment problem. Designs of
diverging degree can amplify a functional by their coordinate count, so whether
a functional survives is a competition between its size and the rate at which
the design's own combinatorics grows -- and the two are linked, because the
design's variance normalization is itself a functional of the panel. Existing
technology answers this one functional at a time, by exhibiting a channel that
reads it. The problem asks for the closure.

Pre-registered failure mode. A proposed generating set will typically be
verified by checking that each generator is readable and that a handful of
non-generators are not. That establishes neither generation nor minimality. Any
claimed answer must be attacked at (b) first.


================================================================================
2. RIGIDITY AND SURGERY: A CHEBYSHEV CRITERION
================================================================================

For a bundle family and a panel, let |U|_* denote the law of the absolute value
of the second-floor variable, and consider the map

    RHO :  mu  |-->  Law(|U|)   ...   (the modulus map)

PROBLEM 2. Characterize the bundle families for which RHO is injective, and for
those where it is not, classify its fibers completely.

The two regimes have entirely different consequences and both occur. When RHO is
injective, matching modulus data pins the panel, and every invariant of the panel
is in principle recoverable from measurements that read only moduli. When RHO has
nontrivial fibers, each fiber is a family of panels agreeing on all modulus data
and differing in odd structure -- a SURGERY FAMILY -- and any measurement class
confined to modulus data cannot separate its members.

For the binomial-2 family RHO is injective, and the mechanism is instructive: the
extreme atom dominates the others in absolute value, is strictly monotone in the
parameter, and therefore the largest modulus present in a panel identifies the
extreme parameter present and localizes its mass to a single bundle; the argument
then recurses. The mass at the extreme atom has nobody to trade with.

What a complete answer contains:
  (a) a necessary and sufficient condition on the atom functions a_j(t) and mass
      functions p_j(t) for injectivity. The natural conjecture is that it is a
      Chebyshev-system condition on the family {|a_j|, p_j} together with a
      DOMINATION-AND-MONOTONICITY hypothesis on the extreme atom, but the
      conjecture is stated here only to be refuted or sharpened;
  (b) when RHO is non-injective: the dimension of the generic fiber, an explicit
      parameterization, and the exact set of functionals constant on fibers;
  (c) the boundary of the two regimes -- a family where injectivity fails
      marginally, and the mechanism of the failure;
  (d) stability: does injectivity survive perturbation of the family? Is the
      rigid locus open, closed, neither?

Why this is hard. Injectivity of RHO is a statement about a nonlinear map on an
infinite-dimensional space, and the finite-dimensional intuition is actively
misleading -- a mixture appears to have enormous freedom, and the theorem is that
the bundling destroys it. The known proof is a peeling argument depending on a
domination property that most families will not have. The general case needs a
criterion, not a peeling.

Pre-registered failure mode. Numerical searches for fibers cluster modulus values
under a tolerance. A tolerance can merge two distinct parameters whose modulus
values agree to high precision while their SIGNS differ, manufacturing an
apparent fiber out of a sign change near a zero of an atom function. Any
computational evidence must be exact-arithmetic, and any claimed fiber must be
exhibited symbolically.

Pre-registered control. The reflection symmetry of a family (when present) makes
RHO non-injective for trivial reasons. A search that does not detect the
reflection fiber is not detecting fibers at all, and a null result from such a
search is uninformative rather than negative.


================================================================================
3. THE NON-CRAMER FRONTIER: A RESONANCE CALCULUS
================================================================================

Every finitely supported law violates Cramer's condition. The characteristic
function of an atomic law is Bohr almost periodic, hence recurrent, so
limsup_{|s| -> infinity} |phi(s)| = 1 exactly, and this holds whether or not the
law is lattice. The lattice/nonlattice distinction, which governs one part of the
existing theory, is irrelevant to Cramer: nonlattice atomic laws fail it by
recurrence rather than by periodicity.

Consequently the entire Edgeworth apparatus -- expansions of window profiles with
remainder o(b^{-1}) or better, uniform over designs -- is unavailable for bundle
families. Every theorem whose proof passes through a smooth-density local limit
expansion is, for these ensembles, unproved.

PROBLEM 3. Build the replacement.

What a complete answer contains:
  (a) a local limit theorem for triangular arrays of parameter-dependent atomic
      laws, with the remainder expressed in terms of the RECURRENCE SET
        S(eps) = { s : |phi_mu(s)| > 1 - eps },
      rather than assumed away;
  (b) a trichotomy by the Diophantine type of the atom-location ratios --
      rationally resonant, Liouville, and Diophantine-generic -- with the sharp
      remainder exponent in each class and examples attaining it;
  (c) a RESONANCE CORRECTION TERM: an explicit series indexed by the near-periods
      of phi, reducing to the classical Edgeworth series when the recurrence set
      is empty at every scale;
  (d) uniformity over designs, stated as a hypothesis with a proof, since it is
      the hypothesis on which everything downstream rests;
  (e) the consequence for insertion-type estimates: whether a difference of two
      panels can be bounded by finitely many matched integral moments as in the
      smooth case, or whether resonance permits leakage that no finite matching
      controls.

Why this is hard. This is genuinely a problem in harmonic analysis and
Diophantine approximation wearing a probabilistic costume. The recurrence set of
an almost periodic function is not small, and its structure depends on the
arithmetic of the atom locations, which for a bundle family VARIES WITH THE
PARAMETER -- so a panel integrates over a continuum of arithmetic types. The
right object may be a measure-theoretic statement about the distribution of
Diophantine types under mu, in which case the answer will look more like an
equidistribution theorem than like a limit theorem.

The payoff, and why this problem is first in importance if not in order: (e) is
the hypothesis under which every invisibility result in this program has been
proved. If resonance permits leakage, invisibility fails exactly for the
ensembles that arise in practice, and the whole picture inverts.


================================================================================
4. THE OBSERVABILITY CORRESPONDENCE
================================================================================

Let I be a complete invariant for a class of objects -- a map separating points.
Let M be a class of admissible measurements, each a functional of the object.
Say a quotient q of I is VISIBLE to M if every pair of objects separated by some
measurement in M is separated by q, and INVISIBLE if no measurement in M
separates two objects that q separates.

There exist pairs (I, M) with I complete and a nontrivial quotient invisible:
an invariant that determines the object and that no admissible measurement can
read. This is the structural phenomenon the rest of this program keeps meeting,
and it deserves a theory rather than instances.

PROBLEM 4. Establish the correspondence between measurement classes and
invariant quotients, in the generality of a Galois connection, and compute it.

What a complete answer contains:
  (a) the two maps -- M |-> its visible quotient, and q |-> the largest
      measurement class blind to q -- shown to form a Galois connection, with
      the closure operators identified on both sides;
  (b) a characterization of the CLOSED measurement classes: which classes are of
      the form "everything blind to q"? Equivalently, when is a blindness
      phenomenon caused by a single invariant rather than by an accident of the
      class?
  (c) the computation of the correspondence for the tower/ladder pair: the
      lattice of quotients of the tower invariant, the lattice of natural
      measurement classes, and the explicit map between them;
  (d) an obstruction theory: given q, a computable certificate that q is
      invisible to M, and a computable certificate that it is visible -- so that
      the question is decidable for the classes of interest;
  (e) the GAP as an object in its own right. When a complete invariant has an
      invisible quotient, the gap is a group or groupoid acting on the objects
      and fixing all measurements. Identify it, and determine when it acts
      freely.

Why this is hard and why it is the most portable problem here. The phenomenon --
a complete invariant with a provably unreadable part -- is not special to this
setting. It is the general shape of a partial-identifiability theorem, and a
correspondence theorem would apply wherever a classification meets a restricted
observation class. The difficulty is that "measurement class" must be given
enough structure to support a Galois theory without being narrowed to the point
where the theory is trivially the subgroup correspondence of an obvious group
action. Finding the right category is most of the problem, and it is the part
that cannot be done by analogy.


================================================================================
5. THE EXACT DEGRADATION LAW
================================================================================

PROBLEM 5. Find the metric.

Conjecture, stated to be proved or refuted: there is a metric D on the space of
panels and a universal increasing function Phi, depending on the accuracy
functional and the design class but NOT on the panels, such that for every
transfer

    degradation(P -> P')  =  Phi( D(P, P') )     EXACTLY,

with the equality cases classified. Prove existence, prove uniqueness of D up to
reparameterization, and compute both D and Phi.

What a complete answer contains:
  (a) D given in closed form as a functional of the two panels, computable from
      inputs;
  (b) the equality cases: which pairs of panels attain the law, and which
      configurations are extremal;
  (c) a proof of uniqueness, or an exact description of the family of metrics
      satisfying the law -- since if D is not unique, the non-uniqueness is
      itself a gauge freedom worth naming;
  (d) the cocycle structure. Degradation along a composite transfer
      P -> P' -> P'' should decompose; identify the cocycle, compute its
      coboundaries, and exhibit the IRREDUCIBLE CLASS -- the part of degradation
      that no reparameterization of the intermediate panel can remove;
  (e) the geodesics of D, and their interpretation as optimal interpolating
      panels.

Why this is hard. An exact law, as opposed to two-sided bounds with matching
order, is a strong demand and most transfer phenomena do not admit one. The
interest is precisely in finding the largest class where the demand is met, so
a complete answer includes the BOUNDARY: a characterization of the design
classes and accuracy functionals for which no exact law can exist, with the
obstruction exhibited.


================================================================================
6. CLASSIFICATION OF ACCURACY FUNCTIONALS BY TRANSFER BEHAVIOR
================================================================================

Different accuracy functionals disagree, under transfer, not merely in magnitude
but in DIRECTION: there exist transfers along which one functional improves and
another degrades. This is usually reported as a caution. It should be a theorem.

PROBLEM 6. Classify accuracy functionals by their behavior under transfer.

What a complete answer contains:
  (a) the equivalence relation "A ~ A' iff A and A' induce the same ordering on
      every pair of panels", and a complete set of invariants for it;
  (b) the classification of functionals by EQUIVARIANCE TYPE under the natural
      group actions -- rescaling of the readout, monotone reparameterization of
      the target, and translation of any threshold the functional depends on --
      with a theorem stating exactly which combinations of equivariance force
      which orderings;
  (c) the THRESHOLD SUBTLETY, isolated as its own theorem. A functional defined
      through a threshold calibrated on the source panel and applied on the
      target is not equivariant under rescaling of the readout, while a
      correlation-type functional is. Determine the exact set of transfers along
      which the two classes disagree in sign, as a function of the readout's
      scale ratio and the target's base rate, and prove it sharp;
  (d) an ORDER-REVERSAL THEOREM: given any two inequivalent functionals,
      construct a transfer on which they disagree in direction, with the
      construction explicit and the minimal such transfer identified;
  (e) the partial order on functionals by "never disagrees in direction with",
      and its maximal chains.

Why this is hard. The classification is not a classification of functionals as
maps -- it is a classification of their level-set geometry relative to the
transfer flow, and two functionals with entirely different formulas can be
equivalent while two nearly identical ones are not. Part (d) in particular asks
for a construction, and constructions in this space have repeatedly turned out
to be obstructed for reasons invisible at the level of the formulas.


================================================================================
7. THE SHARP CEILING ON EXPLAINING A SINGLE RESIDUAL
================================================================================

Let Y = f(X) + noise, let f-hat be an estimate, and consider the squared residual
E = (Y - f-hat(X))^2 as a random variable. Let G be a sub-sigma-algebra
(the "available covariates").

PROBLEM 7. Compute
    C(G) = sup over G-measurable g of the fraction of Var(E) explained by g,
exactly, with the extremal g identified.

This is a sharp-constant problem and it is the correct denominator for a large
class of empirical claims. A reported explained fraction is uninterpretable
without it: the same number can be nearly all of the achievable or nearly none,
and the two readings support opposite conclusions.

What a complete answer contains:
  (a) C(G) in closed form for the ensembles of Section 0, as a functional of the
      panel and the noise structure, with the extremal predictor exhibited;
  (b) the CONDITIONAL NOISE FLOOR: the part of Var(E) that is irreducible for
      information-theoretic rather than modeling reasons, separated cleanly from
      the part that is merely unmodeled;
  (c) the behavior of C(G) as the ensemble size diverges -- in particular whether
      C(G) -> 0, so that ALL predictors of an individual residual become
      asymptotically uninformative, or whether it stabilizes;
  (d) the normalized statistic explained-fraction / C(G), its exact sampling
      distribution or a sharp approximation with rate, and its behavior under
      the transfers of Section 5;
  (e) a comparison theorem: given two sub-sigma-algebras G_1, G_2, exact
      conditions under which the normalized statistics are comparable -- since
      comparing two raw explained fractions against different ceilings is the
      error the whole problem exists to prevent.

Why this is hard. The supremum is over an infinite-dimensional class and the
extremal predictor is generically not the conditional expectation of E, because
E is itself a squared quantity and the objective is a variance ratio rather than
an L2 distance. Sharpness, with the extremal configuration exhibited, is the
whole content; a bound is worth nothing here, because the entire use of the
quantity is as a denominator.


================================================================================
8. THE INVERSE PROBLEM
================================================================================

PROBLEM 8. Given a prescribed degradation profile -- a function assigning to
each panel in some family the degradation from a fixed source -- construct a
bundle family, a source panel, and a weight function realizing it, or exhibit
the obstruction.

What a complete answer contains:
  (a) a characterization of REALIZABLE profiles, as an explicit condition on the
      prescribed function (monotonicity, convexity, growth, and whatever else the
      answer turns out to require);
  (b) a canonical construction realizing every realizable profile, with the
      construction's freedom described -- the gauge group of the realization;
  (c) the obstruction, for non-realizable profiles, as a computable invariant;
  (d) MINIMAL realizations: among all panels realizing a profile, those minimal
      in support size or in some natural complexity, with uniqueness where it
      holds;
  (e) the interaction with Problem 2: on rigid bundle families the realization
      problem has less freedom than on flexible ones, and the exact loss of
      freedom should be quantified rather than described.

Why this is hard. Inverse problems of this type are usually either trivially
solvable by taking enough support or obstructed for cohomological reasons, and
which of the two obtains is not visible from the statement. The demand for
canonical constructions, rather than existence, is what makes this a real
problem: an existence proof by compactness would be a non-answer.


================================================================================
9. CAPSTONE: THE COMPLETE TRANSFER THEOREM
================================================================================

PROBLEM 9. Prove a single theorem of the following shape, or prove that no
theorem of this shape exists and identify precisely what obstructs it.

  For every bundle family satisfying [conditions to be discovered], every
  admissible design class, and every accuracy functional in [class to be
  characterized], there exist finitely many functionals F_1, ..., F_k of the
  panel -- each computable from mu, beta and the family by an explicit integral
  -- and an analytic function Psi such that

      degradation(P -> P')  =  Psi( F_1(P), ..., F_k(P), F_1(P'), ..., F_k(P') )
                               + R,

  with R bounded by an explicit function of the ensemble size, sharp in the
  sense that the exponent is attained; and no set of fewer than k functionals
  suffices.

The theorem must come with:
  (a) the value of k, and the identification of the F_j;
  (b) the domain: exactly which bundle families and which functionals are
      covered, with a counterexample immediately outside it;
  (c) EFFECTIVITY -- the F_j estimable from finite data with stated rates, since
      a law in terms of unobservable functionals resolves nothing;
  (d) the minimality proof, which is the part most likely to be wrong and should
      be attacked first;
  (e) the degenerate cases: which panels make Psi degenerate, and what the law
      says there.

A solution to Problem 9 subsumes the empirical questions this program cares
about, not by addressing them individually but by leaving nothing for them to be
exceptions to. That is the standard: the phenomena should fall out as corollaries
of generality, not be solved one at a time and collected.


================================================================================
10. WHAT COUNTS AS A SOLUTION
================================================================================

For each problem, these are non-negotiable:

EXACTNESS. An inequality is an answer only when accompanied by its equality
cases and a proof of attainment. "Order-sharp" is not sharp.

NECESSITY. A sufficient condition is half a theorem. Every characterization must
run in both directions, and the reverse direction is where the work is.

COMPUTABILITY FROM INPUTS. Every functional appearing in an answer must be an
explicit functional of the given data. A law expressed in terms of quantities
determined by the answer is circular, however analytic.

RATES. Asymptotic statements require rates, and the rates require attainment
examples.

PRE-REGISTERED AUDIT POINTS. Any announced solution must state, IN THE
ANNOUNCEMENT, the two or three steps on which it most depends and which a
skeptical reader should attack first -- typically a uniformity hypothesis, an
interchange of limits, or a step where the hardest case is claimed to reduce to
the easiest. A proof whose author cannot name its weakest point has not been
read carefully enough by its author.

FALSIFIABILITY OF NEGATIVE CLAIMS. A claim that something is impossible, or
invisible, or unreachable, must state what would have to exist for it to be
false. An unfalsifiable impossibility claim is not a theorem; it is a place to
put unfinished work.

CONTROLS ON NULL RESULTS. A search that finds nothing is informative only if it
is known to be capable of finding something. Every computational null must be
accompanied by a positive control -- a configuration where the searched-for
object provably exists and is found.


================================================================================
11. THE SHAPE OF THE ANSWER WE ARE HOPING FOR
================================================================================

The picture that would make this program complete, offered as a target rather
than a conjecture:

Panels form a space carrying a canonical metric. Degradation is an exact function
of that metric. The metric decomposes into finitely many computable coordinates,
which are precisely the reachable algebra of Problem 1. Bundle families split
into a rigid class, where those coordinates determine the panel, and a flexible
class, where the fibers are explicitly parameterized surgeries. The measurement
classes form a lattice in Galois correspondence with quotients of the coordinate
algebra, so that for any class one can compute what it can and cannot see.
Accuracy functionals are classified by their behavior on this structure, and
their disagreements are predicted rather than observed. The whole is effective:
every coordinate is estimable, every rate is stated, and every negative result
names its own refutation.

Every one of the nine problems above is a piece of that picture. None of them is
a step toward it that could be taken by extending an existing argument, which is
the reason they are worth stating.
