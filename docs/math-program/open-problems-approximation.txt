SIX PROBLEMS ON APPROXIMATION, COMPLEXITY, AND OPTIMAL METHOD
=============================================================

A fourth set, disjoint from the previous three. Those asked about spectra of
structured ensembles, deterministic equivalents, estimation over reachable
sets, and the quantitative theory of definitional rigidity. Nothing here
touches any of them.

These are of a different order of ambition, and deliberately so. The model is
not "an open question in the area" but a question whose resolution creates an
area: the existence of non-sofic groups, the Glimm-Effros dichotomy, the
Connes embedding problem. Each asks whether a whole class of objects is
approximable by, classifiable by, or constructible from a simpler class, and
each has the property that BOTH answers are consequential. A yes gives a
machine; a no gives a permanent obstruction and, with it, the knowledge to
stop building machines that cannot exist.

The set is split deliberately along an axis that matters more than the
subject matter.

Problems 1-3 and 6 are OBSTRUCTION problems. They ask what cannot be
approximated, classified, or summarized. This is the kind of question the
surrounding program has been generating on its own, because it is the kind
that failure generates: a conjecture was stated, refuted by a channel nobody
was tracking, restated with the channel added, and refuted again -- three
cycles deep at the time of writing. Several of these ask, in different
settings, whether that process terminates.

Problems 4 and 5 are CONSTRUCTION problems, and they are the ones I would
fund first. They ask not what is impossible but what is optimal: whether the
best procedure for a task can be SYNTHESIZED rather than invented, and whether
one representation of data can serve two purposes at once. Both are questions
about method itself rather than about any particular method. Neither has, so
far as I can determine, been posed as mathematics. They are here because a
program that produces only obstructions eventually knows a great deal about
what cannot be done and nothing about what to do, and because the single most
valuable output either could have -- a procedure nobody would have thought of,
arrived at by solving a variational problem rather than by having an idea --
is not reachable from any obstruction theorem.


------------------------------------------------------------------
STANDING SETUP AND NOTATION
------------------------------------------------------------------

Throughout, nu is a probability law on R with mean 0 and variance 1, and
x_1, x_2, ... are i.i.d. nu.

A *design* of order d is a finite family S_d of subsets of [d] together with
real coefficients (w_S) normalized so that sum_S w_S^2 = 1. It determines the
multilinear form (a *chaos*)

    f_d = sum_{S in S_d} w_S prod_{i in S} x_i .

A sequence of designs is *admissible* if the maximal influence
max_i sum_{S ni i} w_S^2 tends to 0 and the minimum degree tends to infinity.
A design is *disjoint* if its sets are pairwise disjoint. Write L(D, nu) for
the set of subsequential weak limits of f_d, Lambda_disj for the laws so
arising from disjoint designs, and Lambda for the unrestricted analogue.

The *hub energy of multiplicity j* is
H_j(D) = sum_i ( sum_{S ni i} w_S )^j. H_2 governs the first known departure
of Lambda from Lambda_disj; the H_j for j >= 3 are uncharted.

A *nuisance class* K is a convex, weak-*-closed set of positive trace-class
operators. An *experiment* is a family (P_theta)_{theta in Theta} of laws on a
standard Borel space with (theta, A) -> P_theta(A) Borel.

Known and deliberately excluded. The invariance-principle literature (Rotar,
Mossel-O'Donnell-Oleszkiewicz, Nourdin-Peccati-Reinert) settles universality
at BOUNDED degree under vanishing influences and is silent at degree tending
to infinity, the only regime here. Gnedenko-Kolmogorov triangular-array
calculus settles jump-free limits for INDEPENDENT arrays and is exactly what
fails for dependent ones. Harrington-Kechris-Louveau settles the Glimm-Effros
dichotomy for BOREL equivalence relations; Problem 2 is posed where that
hypothesis is unavailable. Le Cam's asymptotic-equivalence theory supplies the
vocabulary of Problems 4 and 5 and, as far as I can determine, none of their
content: it characterizes when procedures TRANSFER between experiments, never
how to produce one.


==================================================================
1. SOFICITY FOR DEPENDENT CHAOS
==================================================================

Sign-erasure phenomena say that for symmetric nu, every design's truncated
second-moment structure is identical to that of its disjoint skeleton: the
overlaps are invisible at second order, at every scale. Fourth order is a
different matter -- there are designs whose fourth joint cumulant survives in
the limit, and whose limit law is consequently a variance mixture rather than
a Gaussian. A design looks disjoint through a second-order probe and does not
look disjoint through a fourth-order one.

That is the shape of a soficity question: an object is locally approximable by
members of a simple class, and one asks whether it is globally a limit of
them.

Definition. Fix r. Two designs are *r-locally equivalent* if all their
limiting joint cumulants of order at most r agree for every admissible nu. A
design sequence is *soficly approximable* if for every r there is a disjoint
design sequence r-locally equivalent to it.

Q1a (soficity). Is every admissible design sequence soficly approximable? A
    negative answer requires a design whose limiting cumulant profile is not
    matched, at some finite order, by ANY disjoint design -- a "non-sofic
    chaos".

Q1b (the spectrum). Determine the closure of Lambda. Two candidate answers are
    sharply different and both defensible: either Lambda-bar is exactly the
    variance mixtures of centred Gaussians, so that dependence buys precisely
    one new degree of freedom and nothing else, or it contains a law that is
    not such a mixture, which is a new limit phenomenon for low-influence
    forms.

Q1c (finiteness of the obstruction theory). Is there a COUNTABLE family of
    design functionals whose limits determine the limit law? The H_j are the
    natural candidates. Prove that (H_j)_{j>=2} with the truncated variance
    determine L(D, nu), or exhibit two admissible sequences agreeing in every
    H_j and in truncated variance whose limit laws differ.

Why it is hard, and why it is the right question. Both sides are live. For
approximability: the sign-erasure argument is exact rather than asymptotic,
and it is not clear what obstructs pushing it to higher order against a
suitably chosen skeleton. Against: the known fourth-order counterexample is a
random hypergraph tuned so that a Poisson overlap law survives in the limit,
and an overlap law is an infinite-dimensional object that a disjoint design
has no room to carry.

Q1c is the operational question. A conjecture of the form "universality holds
iff the coordinate law satisfies conditions C_1, ..., C_k" has been stated and
refuted three times, each refutation supplying a new condition. Q1c asks
whether that sequence terminates. A positive answer turns the conjecture into
a theorem with a finite checklist. A negative answer says no such theorem
exists, which is worth more than a fourth cycle.

What a solution gives. Q1a resolved negatively produces the first object here
that is not a perturbation of an independent one, and an invariant to name it
by. Q1b gives the exact catalogue of limit laws for a class of forms currently
believed to be Gaussian-or-degenerate. Q1c decides whether the classification
programme can succeed at all.


==================================================================
2. DESCRIPTIVE COMPLEXITY IS MONOTONE UNDER GARBLING
==================================================================

Two theories of "how much an experiment tells you" exist and have never been
connected. Le Cam's is quantitative and ordered by garbling: E is more
informative than F if F is a stochastic transform of E. Descriptive set
theory's is qualitative and ordered by Borel reducibility: the observational-
equivalence relation of a family sits somewhere in smooth < E_0 < E_1 <
E_{K_sigma} < analytic, and where it sits determines what kind of invariant
can classify it.

The conjecture is that these orders are compatible.

Definition. For an experiment E, its *observational equivalence* Obs(E) on
Theta is theta ~ theta' iff P_theta = P_theta'. For a Markov kernel M the
*garbling* ME is (M P_theta). Write E >= F when F = ME for some M.

Q2a (monotonicity). Does E >= F imply Obs(E) <=_B Obs(F): can garbling never
    DECREASE descriptive complexity? Discarding information merges fibers, but
    it also changes the parameterization, and Borel reducibility is not
    monotone under arbitrary coarsening.

Q2b (strictness). Exhibit E >= F with Obs(E) smooth and Obs(F) above E_0 -- a
    single garbling that destroys classifiability outright. Conversely, is
    there a deficiency bound: a limit on how far one garbling can raise
    complexity, in terms of M?

Q2c (dichotomy where it is unavailable). Observational equivalence for a
    family that is not Borel-parameterized is analytic, and Glimm-Effros is
    unavailable for analytic equivalence relations. Does the dichotomy
    nevertheless hold for observational equivalence specifically -- is every
    Obs(E) either smooth or above E_0 -- using the special structure (fibers
    of a Borel map into a space of probability measures) that a general
    analytic relation lacks?

Why it is hard. Q2c is a restricted case of the analytic Glimm-Effros problem,
open in general; the bet is that the restriction is what makes it tractable,
since fibers of a Borel map into P(X) are far from arbitrary analytic classes.
Q2a is hard structurally: reducibility asks for a map on PARAMETERS, garbling
acts on DISTRIBUTIONS, and passing between them requires a measurable
selection of parameters from laws -- which is exactly what fails when the
relation is non-smooth. That circularity is the mathematical content.

What a solution gives. A theory in which "this class is unidentifiable"
becomes a quantity trackable through a processing chain rather than a verdict
re-derived at each stage. Q2b's example would be the first demonstration that
a data-reduction step can destroy classifiability -- not accuracy,
classifiability. Monotonicity, if true, is the data-processing inequality for
descriptive complexity and would be used as the ordinary one is.


==================================================================
3. IMITATION AS A CAPACITY
==================================================================

Let K be a nuisance class and consider detecting a rank-one perturbation
against it. The decisive phenomenon is that a perturbation MIXED OVER A PRIOR
can be exactly a member of K: the alternative is then a mixture of nulls, the
experiments are identical, and no test separates them at any sample size. The
obstruction is identifiability, not power, and no amount of estimation,
splitting, or joint modelling touches it.

Definition. The *imitation capacity* is

    t*(K) = sup { lambda^2 : the lambda-mixed rank-one alternative lies in the
                  closed convex hull of the null family } ,

and K is *rigid* if t*(K) = 0. Unconstrained spectral classes have t* bounded
away from 0; classes constrained by a transitive symmetry appear rigid,
because averaging over the symmetry annihilates a delocalized perturbation.

Q3a (variational formula). Express t*(K) via the extreme boundary of K.
    Conjecture: rigidity holds iff the extreme points are the orbit closure of
    a group acting transitively on the relevant sphere, so no extreme point
    can carry a localized perturbation. Prove it, or find a rigid class with
    no such symmetry.

Q3b (duality). Is there a Hahn-Banach certificate: t*(K) = 0 iff some bounded
    linear functional is nonnegative on K and strictly negative on every mixed
    alternative? This converts every adaptive lower bound in the subject into
    an explicit convex-duality computation and every rigidity claim into an
    exhibited certificate.

Q3c (capacitability). Is t* a Choquet capacity -- monotone, continuous along
    increasing sequences, strongly subadditive? If so, capacitability yields
    measurable selection of near-optimal imitators over analytic families of
    nuisance classes: a general machine for composite lower bounds, each of
    which is currently produced by hand.

Why it is hard. Strong subadditivity is a genuine geometric demand -- that the
ability to imitate over a union is controlled by the pieces and their
intersection -- and mixing is global, with no obvious reason to respect it.
Q3a is hard because rigidity is currently established only by explicit
averaging tied to particular symmetries, and a characterization must handle
classes whose extreme boundary is not a single orbit. Underlying both: convex
hulls of covariance classes are badly behaved, and their extreme points are
known in almost no case of interest.

What a solution gives. A criterion, checkable from the class alone, saying
whether adaptation over it is free or impossible -- BEFORE any procedure is
designed. Current practice designs a procedure, fails to prove a guarantee,
and then discovers the obstruction.


==================================================================
4. SYNTHESIS OF OPTIMAL PROCEDURES, AND THE COMPLETENESS OF THE
   LOWER-BOUND CALCULUS
==================================================================

This subject determines optimal rates by a small, fixed catalogue of
lower-bound arguments -- two-point testing, Assouad's cube, Fano and its
metric-entropy relatives -- and determines optimal PROCEDURES by invention.
The asymmetry is total: rates are derived, procedures are thought of. Nobody
knows whether either half is complete, and the question has not been asked in
a form that admits an answer, because "lower-bound method" and "procedure that
someone might think of" are not mathematical objects.

Making them into mathematical objects is the first half of this problem, and
the reason it is on this list.

Definition. Fix a class of *risk problems* -- tuples (Theta, (P_theta), loss,
n) drawn from some effectively presented family -- and write R_n* for minimax
risk. A *certificate calculus* C is a set of maps from problem descriptions to
lower bounds, closed under the operations the subject actually uses:
reduction to a subproblem, convexification, tensorization, and change of
measure. C is *complete* on a class if for every problem in it,
sup_{c in C} c(problem) matches R_n* up to absolute constants.

Q4a (completeness of the calculus). Is the standard calculus complete for
    problems with convex parameter set and convex loss? Or exhibit a problem
    whose true minimax rate is strictly above everything the calculus can
    certify, and PROVE the gap. Proving a gap means proving a statement about
    all certificates at once, which is why this requires the formalization
    above rather than being a question about any one bound.

Q4b (synthesis). Minimax duality identifies the optimal procedure as Bayes
    against a least-favourable prior when one exists. When does it exist, and
    when is it computable from the problem description? Specifically: is there
    a problem whose minimax risk is attained but for which NO optimal
    procedure is computable from the description, in a fixed model of
    computation? That is the exact statement that optimal method cannot be
    synthesized in general, and it would be the first such statement.

Q4c (the synthesis machine, the positive form). Characterize a class of
    problems on which the least-favourable prior is the solution of an
    explicitly computable convex program, so that the optimal procedure can be
    OUTPUT rather than invented. Candidate class: problems whose likelihood
    ratio depends on the nuisance only through a quadratic form -- the same
    condition conjectured in Problem 6 to make thresholds order 1, which is
    either a coincidence or the shadow of a single theorem.

Q4d (what invention adds). If the calculus is incomplete, is the deficit
    itself an invariant? Define the *certification gap* as the ratio of the
    true rate to the best certifiable bound, and ask whether it is bounded on
    natural classes, or whether it can be made arbitrarily large. A bounded
    gap says the toolkit is essentially sufficient; an unbounded one says
    there are problems whose difficulty is invisible to every argument the
    subject knows how to make.

Why it is hard, and why it is worth it. The obstacle is that a negative answer
to Q4a is a meta-theorem: one must determine a true rate by means outside the
calculus and then show the calculus cannot reach it, which requires the
calculus to be closed under enough operations to be worth the name and still
tractable enough to be bounded. That is the same difficulty faced in proving
lower bounds against restricted proof systems, and the techniques there --
interpolation, feasible-interpolation-style arguments, model-theoretic
separations -- are the obvious place to look, though the analogy has not been
attempted. Q4b is hard because computability of a least-favourable prior is a
question about a fixed point of an infinite-dimensional saddle problem, and
the standard existence proofs are non-constructive in a way that has never
been analysed.

What a solution gives. This is the problem whose positive resolution produces
methods that nobody would have thought of. Q4c is literally a machine that
takes a problem and returns the optimal procedure; even a narrow class would
change practice, because the class would be characterized by a checkable
condition rather than by whether anyone has had the idea. Q4a and Q4d answer a
question every practitioner faces and nobody can currently answer: when a
lower bound cannot be proved, is the bound false, or is the toolkit blind?
Those are different situations demanding opposite responses, and at present
they are indistinguishable.


==================================================================
5. SIMULTANEOUS OPTIMALITY: IS THERE A FREE REPRESENTATION?
==================================================================

Data is almost never used for one purpose. It is reduced once -- projected,
whitened, pruned, embedded, summarized -- and the reduction is then used for
detection, for estimation, and for prediction under conditions different from
those it was built under. The reduction is invariably chosen for one of those
tasks, or for none of them, and the cost to the others is not merely unknown
but has no accepted definition.

There is direct evidence the cost is real. For detecting a weak latent
component against a structured background, detectability is governed by a
normalized trace of the INVERSE background, which is dominated by the
directions of smallest variance; reductions that discard low-variance
directions -- the standard, near-universal preprocessing -- discard exactly
the directions carrying detection power. The same reduction is close to
optimal for reconstruction. So two tasks, one representation, opposite
prescriptions.

Definition. A *reduction* is a measurable map T from the sample space to a
second standard Borel space. For a task t with optimal risk R_t(full data),
the *efficiency* of T at t is eff_t(T) = R_t(full)/R_t(T), in [0,1]. A finite
family of tasks is *co-optimizable* if there is a single T with eff_t(T) -> 1
for every t in the family.

Q5a (the uncertainty principle). Exhibit two tasks that are not
    co-optimizable, with a quantitative trade-off: a bound
    Phi(eff_1(T), eff_2(T)) <= 1 valid for every reduction T, with Phi
    explicit and tight. Detection versus reconstruction against a structured
    background is the candidate instance; the conjectured obstruction is the
    angle, in the metric induced by the background, between the directions the
    two tasks weight.

Q5b (classification of the task lattice). Characterize which families of tasks
    ARE co-optimizable. Conjecture: co-optimizability holds iff the tasks'
    influence functions span a common invariant subspace of the nuisance
    operator -- so that the obstruction is a genuine non-commutation, not a
    matter of sample size or of cleverness. If so, co-optimizability becomes
    checkable from the model before any reduction is built.

Q5c (the optimal reduction, single task). Is the optimal reduction for a
    single task always the solution of a convex program over the space of
    Markov kernels? The space of reductions is not convex under composition,
    which is the standard reason to expect no, but the RISK is affine in the
    kernel, which is the standard reason to expect yes. Settling this decides
    whether preprocessing can be optimized rather than chosen.

Q5d (the price of modularity, quantitatively). Define the *modularity gap* of
    a task family as the largest ratio, over families, between the risk of the
    best single shared reduction and the product of the individually optimal
    risks. Is it bounded on natural families? An unbounded modularity gap is
    the statement that shared pipelines are not merely suboptimal but
    unboundedly so, which no result currently asserts or denies.

Why it is hard. Q5a demands a lower bound quantified over ALL measurable
reductions, including non-linear and randomized ones, where the only general
tool is data processing -- and data processing is too coarse, since it bounds
each task separately and says nothing about the trade-off between them. What
is needed is a joint data-processing inequality, an object that does not
exist. Q5c is hard because the natural convexification changes the problem:
the convex hull of reductions is the set of randomized reductions, and whether
randomization helps here is itself open.

What a solution gives. Q5a and Q5d put a number on a decision made constantly
and never analysed: whether to build one representation or several. Q5b makes
the decision checkable in advance. Q5c is the constructive half -- if the
optimal reduction solves a convex program, then preprocessing joins the list
of things that are computed rather than chosen, and the computed answer will
in general not resemble anything currently used. Together with Problem 4 this
is the constructive core of the set: Problem 4 synthesizes the procedure,
Problem 5 synthesizes what it should be given.


==================================================================
6. THE ORDER OF A THRESHOLD
==================================================================

There is a clean theory of the minimal order of a continuous summary
determining a functional: for a functional on a d-dimensional set of
directions, the minimal order of a continuous uniform summary is the dimension
of the span, and the obstruction is topological (invariance of domain) rather
than set-theoretic, since a Borel isomorphism supplies a measurable summary of
order 1.

Detection thresholds are the case that matters, and they behave surprisingly.
For a rank-one signal in noise with column structure K, the threshold is
governed by a SINGLE scalar functional of K -- a normalized trace of the
inverse -- and notably neither by the spectral edge nor by the participation
ratio, the two functionals the subject has historically used. That is an
order-1 summary of an infinite-dimensional nuisance parameter. Whether this is
an accident of the model or an instance of a principle is unknown, and the
answer decides whether "effective dimension" scalars can exist in general.

Q6a (the one-parameter principle). Is there a class of detection problems in
    which the threshold provably has order 1, and can it be characterized?
    Candidate: exactly those problems whose likelihood ratio, after the
    appropriate whitening, depends on the nuisance only through a quadratic
    form. Note this is the same candidate class as Q4c.

Q6b (infinite order). Exhibit a detection problem whose threshold has infinite
    order: two sequences of nuisance structures agreeing in every continuous
    scalar functional from a prescribed rich family, with different
    thresholds. This is the definitive statement that no effective-sample-size
    scalar exists in general.

Q6c (order and topology). Is the order of a threshold a topological invariant
    of the nuisance manifold, computable from the geometry of the class before
    any analysis of the detection problem? The order theory for summaries is
    topological in origin; the conjecture is that thresholds inherit that.

Why it is hard. Q6b requires two structures indistinguishable by a rich family
of functionals but distinguishable by a threshold, and a threshold is defined
by an asymptotic optimization over tests, so the distinguishing quantity has
no closed form. The standard route -- compute both and compare -- is
unavailable exactly where the answer is interesting. Q6a is hard because a
characterization must explain why the two functionals the subject has always
used are both wrong for the one case that is fully solved.

What a solution gives. Q6a is a licence: within the characterized class a
scalar effective-dimension summary exists and can be sought. Q6b is a
prohibition, and prohibitions here have been worth more than licences, because
the effort spent searching for scalar summaries is large and, if Q6b holds,
permanently wasted.


------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT I VOUCH FOR
------------------------------------------------------------------

Relation to existing work. Problem 1 is a soficity question in a setting where
soficity has not been asked; the local-to-global vocabulary is borrowed
deliberately and the analogy is structural, not decorative. Problem 2 asks for
a bridge between Le Cam theory and Borel reducibility that I have not found in
either literature, and its hardest part is a restricted instance of the
analytic Glimm-Effros problem, which is open. Problem 3 treats as a capacity
an object that has not been treated as one; the rigidity it generalizes is
established only in examples. Problem 4's Q4a is, to my knowledge, the first
attempt to ask whether this subject's lower-bound toolkit is COMPLETE, and the
right comparison is proof complexity rather than statistics -- a field where
exactly this question, asked about restricted proof systems, produced decades
of theory. Problem 5 has an adjacent literature (sufficiency, information
bottleneck, sufficient dimension reduction) that treats single tasks; the
joint question, and with it the joint data-processing inequality Q5a needs,
appears absent. Problem 6 extends an order theory that exists for summaries to
thresholds, where it does not.

Two structural remarks that a solver should have. First, the candidate class
in Q4c and the candidate class in Q6a are the same -- likelihood ratios
depending on the nuisance through a quadratic form. Either that is a
coincidence or there is one theorem behind both, and finding it may be easier
than either problem separately. Second, Problems 4 and 5 compose: Q4c
synthesizes the optimal procedure given the data, Q5c synthesizes the optimal
reduction of the data, and the composite question -- synthesize both jointly,
since the optimal reduction depends on the procedure and conversely -- is a
fixed point that neither problem alone poses. I have not stated it as a
seventh problem because I cannot make it precise without an answer to at least
one of its halves, but it is the real target and both halves are worth solving
for it.

I cannot certify that all six are unsolved, and the risk is unevenly
distributed. Q2c is open in the general case with certainty; whether the
restriction to observational equivalence has been studied, I do not know. Q5c
may exist in the sufficient-dimension-reduction or information-bottleneck
literature under different hypotheses and should be searched before serious
effort. Q1a, Q1c, Q3c, Q4a, Q4b and Q6b I would expect to be genuinely open.

Ordering by expected difficulty, hardest first: Q4a, Q1b, Q2c, Q3c, Q6b, Q5a.
Ordering by consequence if solved: Q4c, Q1c, Q5c, Q6b, Q4a, Q3b. Q4c and Q5c
first because they are the only two entries on this list whose solution
produces a method rather than a verdict, and because both are stated so that
a positive answer is a construction one can run. Q1c and Q6b next because both
are cases where the NEGATIVE answer is the more valuable one -- Q1c would end
a classification programme that has failed three times, and Q6b would redirect
a large and currently unfocused search. That two of the four highest-value
entries are valuable for being negative is unusual, and worth stating plainly
to anyone deciding what to attempt.
