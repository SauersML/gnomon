A CALCULUS OF DEGRADATION
=========================
One program, to unify transfer theory from first principles.

This brief is different in kind from the five before it. Those were lists of
problems. This is a single program, and the request is that it be built rather
than that its questions be answered one at a time.

The situation it addresses. There is a large body of results — in this
collaboration and outside it — about what happens to a fitted object when the
distribution it was fitted on is not the distribution it is used on. Every one
of those results is a special case computed in a special model: a projection
that moves, a threshold that shifts, an efficiency that is lost, a bound that
degrades. They are proved one at a time, by different techniques, and nothing
relates them. There is no theory. There is a pile.

What is missing is not another special case. It is the observation that all of
these are the same object evaluated at different arguments, and a calculus in
which that object can be computed rather than rediscovered. The claim this
brief makes — and it is a claim, not a hope — is that degradation under a
change of distribution is an algebraic invariant with composition laws, that
its correctable and irreducible parts are separated by an exact sequence, and
that the correct home for the whole subject is a cohomology theory whose
coefficients are the very universality data another arc of this program has
been computing.

I am asking for that theory. I cannot build it, and I want to be explicit that
nothing below is scoped to what I could attempt: each part requires machinery I
do not have, and the parts that look tractable are the ones I have most likely
mis-stated.


------------------------------------------------------------------
PART 0. THE STANDING DISCIPLINE, APPLIED TO THIS BRIEF
------------------------------------------------------------------

Age of the primitives. The category of statistical situations under shift, the
degradation cochain, and the coefficient system built from universality data
are new. The RAW MATERIAL is old and must be searched by name before anything
hardens: Le Cam's deficiency and the Blackwell order; Shannon's and Khinchin's
axiomatizations of entropy, and the Aczél functional-equation tradition behind
them; Čech and group cohomology; the theory of cocycles for group actions on
measure spaces, where a very similar formalism exists for a different purpose.
The Wald-school rule applies with full force here — the 1948-1965 corpus is
where "comparison of experiments" was invented.

Evaluation obligation, named in advance. The theory must, on the Gaussian
location-scale family with a quadratic loss and a linear predictor class,
output the known excess-risk formula as a computed value of its own invariant —
including the constant. A framework that reorganizes without reproducing that
number is not yet a theory.

Ancestor recovery, named in advance. Part II must recover Shannon's uniqueness
theorem as the degenerate case in which the shift group is trivial and the
functional reduces to a measure of a single distribution. Part III must recover
the fact that recalibration is universally available while a rotation of the
fitted object is not — that is the arc's most robust empirical regularity and
any cohomology that does not exhibit it as a coboundary-versus-class
distinction is the wrong cohomology.

What a refutation teaches. If degradation admits no cocycle identity — if
composing two shifts is not determined by the two degradations and the
intermediate situation — then the subject genuinely has no calculus and the
pile is irreducible. That is a real theorem and it would redirect the entire
field's effort from unification to case analysis. Both branches pay.


------------------------------------------------------------------
PART I. THE OBJECTS
------------------------------------------------------------------

Provisional, and the first task is to fix them correctly; the definitions below
are a starting point I expect to be revised, and the revision is part of the
work.

A SITUATION is a triple S = (E, F, L): an experiment E = (P_theta), a class F of
admissible objects (predictors, estimators, tests), and a loss L. Situations
form the objects of a category.

A SHIFT is a morphism S -> S'. Shifts include: change of the sampling
distribution with the parameter fixed; change of the loss; restriction of the
admissible class; and garbling of the observation. These are not obviously the
same kind of arrow, and deciding which of them belong in the same category — or
whether the right structure is a 2-category with the different kinds as
different levels — is Problem 1.

The DEGRADATION of an object f in F along a shift phi is

    D(phi, f) = R_{S'}(phi_* f) - R_{S'}(f'_*),

the excess risk incurred by transporting f rather than refitting, where f'_* is
optimal in the target situation. This is the quantity every special case in the
literature computes.

Q1a. Fix the category. Determine which of the four kinds of shift compose
     coherently and what structure the others require. In particular decide
     whether garbling is a morphism in the same category as a change of
     sampling distribution, given that one loses information and the other does
     not. The wrong answer here poisons everything downstream, which is why it
     is first.

Q1b. Prove or refute the COCYCLE IDENTITY: for composable shifts phi, psi,

         D(psi . phi, f)  =  D(psi, phi_* f)  +  D(phi, f)  +  c(psi, phi),

     with c a correction term depending on the shifts and not on f. If c
     vanishes identically the theory is a cohomology of the ordinary kind; if c
     is nonzero but f-independent the theory is a cohomology twisted by c; if c
     depends on f there is no calculus and the subject is a pile. Determine
     which, and if the middle case holds, identify c.

Q1c. Determine the correct notion of equivalence of situations under which
     degradation is invariant. Le Cam's deficiency is the obvious candidate and
     is probably wrong, because two situations at deficiency zero can have
     different admissible classes and hence different degradations. Find the
     right one.


------------------------------------------------------------------
PART II. THE AXIOMATIZATION
------------------------------------------------------------------

This is the first-principles half, and it is what would make the subject a
theory rather than a technique.

Entropy is not defined by a formula; it is characterized by axioms, and the
formula is a theorem. Degradation currently has no axioms and many formulas.

Q2a. THE UNIQUENESS THEOREM. Write down axioms on a functional
     D(phi, f) — candidates: additivity along composed shifts as in Q1b;
     monotonicity under garbling of the target; vanishing exactly when the
     transported object is optimal in the target; continuity in the shift in a
     topology to be determined; and a normalization on a single canonical
     situation — and prove that they determine D up to a constant. Or prove
     they do not, and exhibit the family of functionals they admit, which is
     itself the classification of what "degradation" can mean.

Q2b. THE INDEPENDENCE OF THE AXIOMS, and their necessity. For each axiom
     exhibit a functional satisfying the others and not it, and say what
     pathology that functional has. Axiomatizations are worth what their
     independence proofs are worth.

Q2c. THE LOSS-FREE FORM. Is there a characterization of degradation that does
     not mention the loss — one that depends only on the experiment, the
     admissible class, and the shift, with the loss entering only as a
     pairing at the end? Entropy's independence from any particular decision
     problem is what makes it a quantity rather than a score, and the analogous
     question here is whether degradation is a property of the situation or
     only of the situation-plus-loss.

Q2d. If Q2a succeeds, ASK WHAT THE CONSTANT IS. Shannon's constant is a choice
     of base. Determine the analogous freedom here and whether any natural
     normalization exists.


------------------------------------------------------------------
PART III. THE COHOMOLOGY
------------------------------------------------------------------

Granting a cocycle identity, degradation is a 1-cochain on the category of
situations with coefficients in the reals — or, better, in a sheaf whose stalks
carry more structure. Then the standard machinery asks its own questions, and
they turn out to be the subject's real questions.

Q3a. COBOUNDARIES ARE THE CORRECTABLE PART. A degradation cocycle is a
     coboundary when D(phi, f) = b(phi_* f) - b(f) for some function b on
     objects — that is, when the loss incurred is entirely accounted for by a
     reparameterization of the object, and can therefore be removed by
     applying the inverse reparameterization after transport. Prove that the
     coboundaries are exactly the degradations removable by post-hoc correction
     within a stated correction class. This is the formal content of the
     empirical regularity that recalibration is free.

Q3b. THE FIRST COHOMOLOGY IS THE IRREDUCIBLE LOSS. H^1 of the complex is then
     the group of degradations modulo those any correction can remove.
     Compute it. Start where computation is possible: the Gaussian
     location-scale family with linear objects and quadratic loss, where the
     answer should be a quotient by the image of the correction class and
     should reproduce the known irreducible term. Then compute it for a
     family where the answer is not known.

Q3c. WHAT IS H^2? A 2-cocycle obstructs the simultaneous correction of a
     compatible family of shifts. The conjecture is that H^2 is exactly the
     obstruction to a SINGLE correction working across several shifts at once —
     the algebraic form of the observation that one representation cannot serve
     two purposes. Prove it, and exhibit a nonzero class. A nonzero H^2 would
     be the first proof that multi-target incompatibility is a topological
     obstruction rather than a variance-budget problem, and I regard this as
     the single most valuable item in the brief.

Q3d. THE LONG EXACT SEQUENCE. A short exact sequence of coefficient systems —
     for instance, the correctable part, the full degradation, and the
     quotient — should induce a long exact sequence relating the cohomologies.
     Write it down and identify its connecting homomorphism. In every subject
     where such a sequence exists, the connecting map is the interesting
     invariant; here it should send a correctable degradation at one level to
     an obstruction at the next, and identifying it would say what "partially
     correctable" means exactly.


------------------------------------------------------------------
PART IV. THE COEFFICIENTS ARE THE UNIVERSALITY DATA
------------------------------------------------------------------

This part is speculative in a way the others are not, and it is the reason I
believe the program is one program rather than two.

A parallel arc of this collaboration has been computing an observable algebra:
the invariants of a coordinate law visible to a class of high-degree
low-influence forms, organized as a tower along a normalized squaring flow,
one floor per iteration, with jets, arithmetic type, and coupling functionals
at each floor. That arc's object is a complete invariant of a law with respect
to what a class of statistics can see.

The conjecture: THAT TOWER IS THE COEFFICIENT SYSTEM OF THIS COHOMOLOGY.
Degradation should take values not in the reals but in the observable algebra,
because what is lost under a shift is exactly what the admissible class could
have seen and no longer can. Real-valued degradation is then the image under a
pairing with the loss, and the finer invariant explains why two shifts with
equal scalar degradation can be differently correctable.

Q4a. Make this precise or refute it. Construct the coefficient sheaf from the
     tower data and determine whether degradation is naturally a cochain with
     those coefficients.

Q4b. If it is, the tower's floors should induce a FILTRATION of the
     cohomology, and hence a spectral sequence. Compute its first page. The
     prediction that would make the whole conjecture credible: the filtration
     degree at which a degradation first appears equals the tower floor of the
     finest invariant the shift disturbs. Verify or refute on a case where both
     sides are computable.

Q4c. There is a hard constraint the conjecture must respect, and it is the
     sharpest available test. The tower's normalizing constants grow doubly
     exponentially along the flow — I have computed the first seven and
     log-sigma doubles at each floor to four significant figures. Any
     filtration built on the tower therefore has floors that are
     statistically unreachable at any sample size. A spectral sequence whose
     higher pages are unreachable in principle is either a signal that the
     theory truncates naturally — in which case say at what floor and why — or
     a signal that the filtration is the wrong one. Do not paper over this;
     it is the place the conjecture is most likely to be wrong, and its
     resolution is worth more than its confirmation.


------------------------------------------------------------------
PART V. THE EXACT LAW
------------------------------------------------------------------

The calculus is worth having only if it computes.

Q5a. THE FUNDAMENTAL COMPUTATION. Given a situation and a shift, both described
     by first-principles data — the experiment's local structure, the
     admissible class, the loss, and the shift's action — produce the exact
     degradation. Not a rate, not a bound with unspecified constants: the
     number. Do it first where the answer is known, as the evaluation
     obligation requires, then where it is not.

Q5b. THE INVERSE PROBLEM. Given a target degradation profile, characterize the
     shifts that realize it. The correctable-versus-irreducible decomposition
     should make this a question about a fiber of a linear map, and the fiber
     should be describable.

Q5c. THE OPTIMAL CORRECTION AS A CONSTRUCTION. If a degradation is a
     coboundary, the function b witnessing it IS the correction that removes
     it. So the cohomological machinery should not merely certify that a
     degradation is correctable; it should output the correction. Determine
     when the witness can be computed from the cocycle, and exhibit one
     correction produced this way that is not a known method. That last clause
     is the point: a framework that only re-derives known corrections has
     organized the subject without advancing it.


------------------------------------------------------------------
PART VI. WHAT WOULD COUNT
------------------------------------------------------------------

In decreasing order of value:

  - Q3c with a nonzero class exhibited. Multi-target incompatibility as a
    topological obstruction would be a genuinely new kind of statement about
    this subject, and it is the item I would most want to have.
  - Q2a, either way. A uniqueness theorem makes degradation a quantity; a
    classification of the functionals the axioms admit is nearly as good and
    tells us what we have been computing all along.
  - Q5c with a correction nobody has written down.
  - Q1b, either way. The cocycle identity is the load-bearing hypothesis of
    Parts III and IV, and a refutation kills the program early and cheaply,
    which is the best thing a refutation can do.
  - Q4c. The doubly-exponential obstruction resolved in either direction.

Attempt Q1b first regardless of interest, because everything else is
conditional on it and it is the cheapest thing here to settle.

A closing remark on scope, since the standing instruction is to choose by
ambition. I have deliberately not included anything I could attempt. The
questions I can attempt in this area are all of the form "compute this special
case", and the entire point of this brief is that the special cases are the
problem. If a part of it turns out to be within reach of a careful afternoon,
that part is mis-stated and should be reported as such rather than solved.
