OPEN PROBLEMS FOR THE PGS PORTABILITY THEORY
============================================

What follows is a list of results that are currently NOT established, ordered
roughly by impact. The organizing principle: at each point where the present
theory takes something as a hypothesis, an assumed constant, or a free
parameter, there is a theorem that ought to replace it. Those are the real
open problems. A few are open research questions in their own right
(random matrix theory, minimax transfer learning); the rest are hard but
almost certainly provable with enough work.

Difficulty is marked [RESEARCH] (genuinely open / publishable in its own
field), [HARD] (a serious project, but the path is visible), or
[TRACTABLE] (mostly labor).


------------------------------------------------------------------
TIER 1 — THE ONES THAT WOULD CHANGE WHAT THE THEORY CAN CLAIM
------------------------------------------------------------------

1. A BBP PHASE TRANSITION FOR REAL GENOTYPE MATRICES          [RESEARCH]

   The correctability calculus rests on comparing a rank-one demographic
   spike 2*F*m_eff against a threshold sqrt(n/M). This is the classical
   Baik-Ben Arous-Peche boundary for spiked covariance with i.i.d. entries.
   Genotype matrices are not i.i.d.: columns are correlated by linkage
   disequilibrium, entries are {0,1,2} rather than Gaussian, and per-locus
   variance is 2p(1-p) before standardization.

   Needed: a phase transition theorem for a spiked model whose noise has a
   general LD covariance, together with an eigenvector-overlap bound above
   the threshold. Equivalently: prove that "effective independent marker
   count M" is the correct summary of a dependent panel, and derive it from
   the LD structure rather than positing it.

   Why it matters: this is the single assumption load-bearing for every
   claim about which population structure is detectable and correctable.
   Without it, the calculator is a heuristic with a proof attached to its
   algebra but not to its premise.

   Related open sub-question: below-threshold spikes are invisible to the
   sample eigenvectors, but are they invisible to *every* estimator, or
   only to PCA? A minimax lower bound for detecting sub-threshold structure
   would turn "PCA cannot correct this" into "nothing can."


2. THE CORRECT SUFFICIENT STATISTIC FOR INDIVIDUAL-LEVEL ACCURACY  [HARD]

   Open Question 1 in the literature is that genetic distance poorly
   predicts individual accuracy. The present theory proves the negative
   direction: scalar summaries are insufficient, and one can exhibit two
   configurations with identical source R-squared and different portability.

   The positive theory is missing. What function of (individual genotype,
   source LD, target LD, effect vector) does predict individual accuracy?
   Candidate: a local, individual-specific quantity such as the quadratic
   form of the weight-error vector against the individual's own genotype
   covariance neighborhood. The goal is a theorem of the form

       E[(Yi - PGSi)^2 | Gi] = f(explicit local quantities)

   with f computable from data, plus a proof that no lower-dimensional
   summary suffices.

   Why it matters: this converts the field's central negative finding into
   an actionable estimator. It is the highest-value item on this list.


3. MINIMAX RATES FOR PREDICTION UNDER COVARIANCE SHIFT          [RESEARCH]

   Given n_source labeled samples from population S and n_target from T,
   with covariance shift Sigma_S -> Sigma_T and effect shift beta_S ->
   beta_T, what is the minimax optimal excess target risk, and which
   estimator attains it?

   The present theory has exact risk decompositions and an upper bound
   R2_target <= rg^2 * R2_source, but no lower bound over a class of
   problems and no optimality statement for any estimator. The transfer
   learning literature has analogous results under posterior-drift and
   covariate-shift models; the genetics case is distinctive because the
   shift is not arbitrary — it is generated by a demographic process, so
   the parameter class is constrained by coalescent theory.

   Concretely: define the class of (Sigma_S, Sigma_T, beta_S, beta_T)
   reachable by t generations of drift at effective size Ne with
   recombination rate r, and compute the minimax rate over that class.
   A rate that depends on F_ST and t rather than on an abstract divergence
   would be a genuinely new result.


4. SAMPLE COMPLEXITY OF RECALIBRATION                            [HARD]

   The theory establishes a clean qualitative hierarchy: mean shifts are
   recoverable, slope changes are recoverable, LD mismatch is NOT linearly
   recoverable, and effect turnover requires target data. This is the most
   practically useful thing the corpus says.

   What is missing is the quantitative version. How many target samples are
   required to restore calibration to within epsilon, as a function of
   F_ST, the number of variants, and the degree of effect turnover? Both
   directions are open: an achievability result for an explicit
   recalibration procedure, and a lower bound showing no procedure does
   better.

   Why it matters: this is the number a study designer actually wants.
   "You need roughly N target samples for this ancestry" is the deliverable.


------------------------------------------------------------------
TIER 2 — DERIVING THE FREE PARAMETERS
------------------------------------------------------------------

5. EFFECT-SIZE TURNOVER FROM AN EXPLICIT SELECTION MODEL         [HARD]

   Cross-population effect correlation (rho, or rg) is currently a free
   parameter. Everything downstream of it is conditional. Deriving rg from
   a mechanistic model — stabilizing selection with pleiotropy, or
   fluctuating selection with a moving optimum, at strength s over t
   generations — would close the largest remaining gap between the
   demographic layer and the prediction layer.

   Target theorem: rg = g(s, t, Ne, pleiotropy degree), with the immune-
   trait case (fast decay) and the anthropometric case (slow decay) as
   corollaries rather than as separate assumptions.


6. THE MULTI-LOCUS COALESCENT COVARIANCE OPERATOR              [HARD]

   Present coverage is the two-locus case with a discrete recombination
   survival factor, from which a covariance-gap lower bound follows. The
   general object — the full genotype covariance operator induced by the
   ancestral recombination graph under a given demography, and how it maps
   to LD decay at every scale — is not derived.

   With it, the F_ST-dependence of LD mismatch would be a theorem rather
   than a modeling choice, and the covariance gap would be computable from
   demographic history instead of bounded below by a witness.


7. EXACT NON-GAUSSIAN SCORE DISTRIBUTION                       [TRACTABLE]

   Metrics (AUC, R-squared) are currently certified as intervals around a
   Gaussian center, with radius given by an assumed Berry-Esseen constant.
   The constant is a parameter; the Berry-Esseen theorem for a weighted sum
   of Hardy-Weinberg genotypes is not itself proved, and no sharper
   expansion is available.

   Two improvements, in increasing order of value:
     (a) prove the Berry-Esseen bound for this specific ensemble, fixing
         the constant;
     (b) an Edgeworth expansion, which would give skewness corrections —
         these matter precisely in the tails, which is where clinical
         risk thresholds live.

   The tail behavior is the point. A Gaussian approximation that is
   excellent at the center can be badly wrong at the 99th percentile,
   which is the only percentile anyone screens on.


8. HIGH-DIMENSIONAL TRANSPORT (p > n)                          [RESEARCH]

   The master transport decomposition assumes an invertible covariance
   matrix, supplied as a hypothesis. Real polygenic scores use hundreds of
   thousands to millions of variants with far fewer samples, so the
   covariance is singular and the "optimal weights" object does not exist
   as written.

   Needed: the transport identity for ridge-regularized or pseudo-inverse
   estimators, and a proportional-asymptotics analysis (p/n -> gamma) of
   how the portability gap behaves. Random matrix theory for ridge
   regression under covariance shift is an active area; the genetics
   version with LD-structured design is open.


------------------------------------------------------------------
TIER 3 — GAPS THAT ARE NARROWER BUT STILL REAL
------------------------------------------------------------------

9. WHEN IS THE rg^2 BOUND TIGHT?                              [TRACTABLE]

   R2_target <= rg^2 * R2_source is proved, and equality is shown in the
   proportional-effects, diagonal-LD case. The general characterization is
   missing: what is the exact gap as a functional of the LD mismatch, and
   which configurations saturate the bound? A sharp two-sided result would
   tell practitioners when rg is a usable proxy for portability and when
   it is badly optimistic.

10. NON-ADDITIVE TRANSPORT                                      [HARD]

   Everything is L2 and linear. Dominance and epistasis change the additive
   effect sizes themselves when allele frequencies shift — Fisher's average
   effect is frequency-dependent, which the corpus notes but does not
   propagate through the transport machinery. A transport theorem for a
   model with dominance would quantify how much of the observed portability
   gap is an artifact of forcing an additive model onto a non-additive
   architecture.

11. A QUANTITATIVE FAIRNESS FRONTIER                            [TRACTABLE]

   The impossibility results are qualitative: you cannot simultaneously
   satisfy calibration and equalized odds across populations with unequal
   R-squared. The quantitative version — the exact Pareto frontier of
   achievable (calibration error, equalized-odds violation) pairs as a
   function of the portability gap — would let a deployment argue for a
   specific operating point rather than merely acknowledging a conflict.

12. OPTIMAL FREQUENCY-RESOLVED ESTIMATION                       [HARD]

   Differentiation-matched weighting M_c * F_c is proved optimal within a
   restricted family of weightings. Whether it is minimax optimal over all
   estimators of recent structure, given per-class marker counts and
   differentiation, is open. This is the design-calculator analogue of
   item 1's lower-bound question.

13. THE WINNER'S-CURSE / ALLELIC-TURNOVER INTERACTION           [TRACTABLE]

   Both effects are treated separately: ascertainment bias vanishes
   asymptotically in signal strength (proved), and turnover degrades
   portability (proved). Their interaction is asserted to be amplifying but
   not derived. An exact formula for the joint effect would matter because
   the two are confounded in every real GWAS.


------------------------------------------------------------------
A NOTE ON WHAT WOULD MAKE ANY OF THIS PUBLISHABLE
------------------------------------------------------------------

Items 1, 3, and 8 are the ones a probability or statistics audience would
recognize as open problems independent of genetics. Items 2 and 4 are the
ones a genetics audience would care most about. Item 2 is the one that
would most change practice, because it is the only item on this list whose
solution is directly an estimator that someone could run on a cohort
tomorrow.

The common structural weakness across the whole theory is that the
demographic layer (coalescent, drift, F_ST) and the prediction layer (risk,
calibration, metrics) are joined by parameters — rg, the covariance gap,
the effective marker count — that are currently assumed rather than
derived. Items 1, 5, and 6 are all instances of closing that seam. Doing
any one of them would upgrade a large number of downstream conditional
results into unconditional ones.
