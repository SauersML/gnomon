RESPONSE: THE IMMUNITY TEST, AND FIVE HARDER TARGETS
====================================================

On the Problem 4 kill, the lesson it actually teaches, and what to attempt next.


------------------------------------------------------------------
PART I. THE KILL WAS CORRECT AND IS THE BEST OUTPUT SO FAR
------------------------------------------------------------------

Filed early, conceded completely, and extended one strike further than the
critic reached — Karlin's total-positivity theory kills the Gaussian instance
that the next session was steering toward. That extension is the part that
matters. Anyone can accept a refutation; extending someone else's kill list
against your own work is a different thing, and it is the behaviour that makes
the rest of this collaboration worth running.

Two protocol amendments were adopted and both are right: the Wald-school corpus
is searched by name before anything about decision primitives, and a bipolar
statement is not a law because a law must EVALUATE its dual side rather than
quantify over it. Keep both.

But the diagnosis stops one level short, and the missing level is the whole
lesson.

The failure was not that the searches aimed at the wrong flank. It was that the
PRIMITIVE OF THE PROBLEM WAS CLASSICAL. The achievable power body is the risk
set; the risk set has been the primitive of statistical decision theory since
1950; and any question whose subject is a classical primitive has, with near
certainty, a mid-century literature, because the people who introduced the
primitive spent a decade characterizing it. No search discipline fixes this,
because the problem is upstream of searching. A better search would have found
Dantzig-Wald faster; it would not have made the problem worth attempting.

So the amendment I want added is not about searching. It is about selection.


------------------------------------------------------------------
PART II. THE IMMUNITY TEST
------------------------------------------------------------------

Before any campaign opens, the target passes four checks. These are cheap and
they are to be applied in writing, in the session log, before the first
theorem.

1. AGE OF THE PRIMITIVE. Name the objects the problem quantifies over. If every
   one of them could have been written down in 1955, expect that it was, and
   expect the interesting structure to have been extracted. This is not a
   prohibition — classical primitives can host genuinely open questions — but it
   raises the bar: you must name the specific thing about your question that the
   people who owned the primitive could not have asked. "Nobody assembled these
   pieces" is not such a thing. Assembly is what expositions do.

   The Design Functor passes this cleanly, and the reasoning given for it is
   correct: its raw material is No-Jump, Sign-Erasure, Transmission, DNC-prime,
   the spectrum theorem and the anti-checklist theorem, and the question it asks
   is the one the anti-checklist theorem leaves open. That cannot be 1951'd
   because the 1951 corpus has no access to the objects.

2. THE EVALUATION OBLIGATION. This generalizes the bipolar tell and should
   replace it, because the bipolar tell is a special case and the general form
   catches more. Before attempting a law, NAME IN ADVANCE a specific instance on
   which the law must produce a number, a law, or an object. Write the instance
   into the session log. A statement that cannot be made to output something on
   a named instance is a characterization, not a law, no matter how it is
   phrased — and the failure mode is not always a bipolar theorem. It can be an
   existence statement, a fixed-point characterization, or a variational formula
   whose optimization is as hard as the original problem. The membership "law"
   failed this test; so, quietly, would have a minimax-duality "synthesis"
   theorem that identifies the optimal procedure as Bayes against an
   uncomputable prior.

   Section 1 of the new campaign already meets this obligation and says so —
   window values, transmission constants, spectral moments. Keep that discipline
   visible in every future document; it is the cheapest possible defence against
   the class of error just committed.

3. ANCESTOR RECOVERY, WITH THE ANCESTOR NAMED FIRST. Every proposed
   generalization must recover a known theorem as a degenerate case, and the
   ancestor must be identified BEFORE the general statement is proved, not
   after. Identifying it afterwards is how a reformulation gets mistaken for a
   generalization: you find the ancestor, observe that you recover it, and take
   that as confirmation rather than as the warning it usually is. If the
   ancestor is recovered and nothing else is gained, the work is exposition.

   The quadratic-sector recovery of the eigenvalue multiset is the right shape:
   ancestor named, recovery checked, and the general statement says strictly
   more.

4. THE FAILURE MODE MUST BE INFORMATIVE. State, in advance, what a refutation
   would teach. Problems whose negative answer is "no such law exists, and we
   learn nothing about why" are worth less than their difficulty suggests. The
   K3 block-normal-form conjecture is exemplary here — the log already records
   that a failure "names a new sector," so both branches pay.

Applied to the record: Problem 4 failed test 1 outright and test 3 in
substance. Problem 1 passes all four. The tests are not hard to apply, which is
the point.

PART III. ON THE DESIGN FUNCTOR CAMPAIGN
------------------------------------------------------------------

Continue it. Three remarks, in decreasing order of confidence.

First: the vertex-weight law is the strongest thing in the session, stronger
than the Functor Law itself, and it deserves to be stated as a theorem in its
own right rather than as support for Q1b. "The constants of the coordinate law
that can enter any diagram density are exactly the two-jet, the arithmetic
type, the symmetry, and the cumulant sequence of the squared coordinate" is a
completeness statement about what is observable, derived as the range of an
explicit map. That is a law in the strict sense demanded above: it evaluates,
and it forecloses. Separate it, name it, and give it its own ancestor check.

Second: K1's adversary hunt is correctly prioritized, and I would sharpen the
target. The danger named is oscillation in tau. The sharper question is whether
the tau-diagonal can be made to carry information that no fixed tau carries —
i.e. whether the limit is a genuine function of the diagonal rather than of the
family of fixed-tau limits. If it is, the Functor Law needs the diagonal in its
statement and the classification becomes a statement about a limit of a family
rather than a family of limits. That is a real distinction and it should be
settled before the determinacy proof, not after, because it changes what is
being proved.

Third, a caution about K2. Truncation repairs compactness for the truncated
data, which is not the same as repairing it for the design. If the limit object
is a consistent family of measures on configuration types, then two designs
with the same limit object may still differ in ways that no truncation sees —
and by the anti-checklist theorem, profile-blindness is already known to be a
real phenomenon in this subject. Before proving compactness, check whether the
proposed limit object separates the two circulant lifts. If it does not, the
object is too coarse and the classification will be vacuous in exactly the way
the earlier checklist was.

PART IV. FIVE HARDER TARGETS, EACH IMMUNE BY CONSTRUCTION
------------------------------------------------------------------

Chosen so that the primitive is not classical. For each I state the immunity
argument first, because that is now the entry requirement, and the evaluation
obligation second.


1. THE COMPLETENESS OF THE MECHANISM LIST
   ("there is no fourth channel", made into a theorem)

The program has found three channels through which a coordinate law becomes
visible to admissible designs: window functionals, sign couplings, and hub
transmission. Each was found by a refutation of a conjecture that the previous
list was complete. The pattern has run three times.

THE TARGET. Prove a classification: there are exactly N channels, and here they
are. Formally — the map from coordinate laws to the family of achievable design
limits factors through exactly the invariants named in the vertex-weight law,
and every one of them is exposed by some admissible design. Equivalently: the
vertex-weight law's list is complete AND exposed, so no fourth mechanism can
exist because there is no fourth invariant for it to transmit.

IMMUNITY. The channels are this program's objects; the classification cannot
predate them. The vertex-weight law has already reduced the conjectural half to
a range computation, which is why this is now attemptable rather than
aspirational.

EVALUATION OBLIGATION. On the chameleon stratum, the theorem must output the
complete list of designs that separate a given chameleon from the Gaussian, and
the list must be finite and computable from the law's constants.

WHY IT IS THE MOST VALUABLE ITEM HERE. It ends the state-refute-restate cycle
by making the cycle's termination the theorem. Three refutations is enough
evidence that guessing the list does not work; the vertex-weight law is the
first tool that could settle it instead.


2. THE EXACT COMPUTATIONAL EXCHANGE RATE

The Q4a campaign graded certificates by prior complexity and proved a modulus
law: completeness is grade-insensitivity, and the deficit is a modulus ratio.
The extension already logged is to grade computationally. Do it, and demand
exactness.

THE TARGET. For a problem and a computational budget B, an exact law giving the
optimal achievable rate as an explicit functional of a COMPLEXITY MODULUS,
defined so that: the unbounded-budget limit recovers the statistical minimax
rate; the low-degree, statistical-query, and sum-of-squares hardness families
each appear as evaluations of the same modulus at particular budgets; and the
exchange rate between budget and rate is a computable function of the problem
description, not merely a bound.

IMMUNITY. Computation is not a mid-century statistical primitive, and the
modulus formulation is this program's own. The existing literature proves
hardness against fixed adversary classes one at a time; a single modulus whose
evaluations reproduce them is a different object.

EVALUATION OBLIGATION. It must reproduce, as evaluations, at least two known
computational thresholds with their constants, and must output a prediction for
one problem where the threshold is currently unknown.

RISK, STATED HONESTLY. The low-degree conjecture has fallen twice recently, so
the field's own organizing conjecture is unstable. That is an argument FOR this
target rather than against it: a modulus that explains why the conjecture holds
where it holds, and fails where it fails, is exactly what a subject in that
state needs.


3. EXACTNESS WHERE THE SUBJECT HAS ONLY ASYMPTOTICS

Nearly every result in high-dimensional limit theory is asymptotic with
unspecified constants, and the constants are what decide applications. The
program has already produced one exception: an exact finite-n computation via
inverse-Wishart moments, valid at every n with no random matrix theory.

THE TARGET. Characterize exactly which ensembles admit an EXACT finite-n law —
a closed form valid at every n, not an asymptotic with an error term — and give
the law where it exists. Conjecture: exactness is equivalent to an invariance
property of the ensemble under a group acting on the design, so that exact
laws exist precisely where a representation-theoretic decomposition is
available, and the asymptotic regime is what remains after averaging destroys
it.

IMMUNITY. The question is not "what is the limit" but "when is there no limit
needed", which the asymptotic literature does not ask because its whole
apparatus presumes the answer is no.

EVALUATION OBLIGATION. Output the exact finite-n law for one ensemble where
only an asymptotic is currently known, with the constant.

WHY IT MATTERS BEYOND ITS STATEMENT. Every exact law is a test oracle for the
asymptotic theory: a case where the answer is known at every n is a case where
an asymptotic claim can be checked rather than believed.


4. THE SYNTHESIS FUNCTOR, WITH THE OBLIGATION ENFORCED

The composite-pipeline collapse showed that procedure and reduction are one
convex program under shared randomness, dissolving an apparent fixed point.
That is a genuine structural result and it survives the kill. What it does not
yet do is output a procedure.

THE TARGET. A map from model description to procedure that terminates and
produces an object. Not "the optimizer is Bayes against the least-favourable
prior" — that is the failure mode test 2 exists to catch. The demand is: name a
class where the least-favourable prior is the solution of an explicitly
solvable program, solve it, and exhibit the resulting procedure. Then determine
the exact boundary of that class.

IMMUNITY. The convex-program formulation with a bottleneck and shared
randomness is not classical, and the nonnegative-rank obstruction that makes
the unshared version intractable is a post-1980 object.

EVALUATION OBLIGATION. Exhibit one procedure that is optimal, that the
construction produced, and that no one would have written down. If the
construction only ever returns known procedures, the class was chosen too
small and the result is a rederivation.

THIS IS THE ONE MOST LIKELY TO PRODUCE A METHOD. It is also the one where the
temptation to produce a characterization instead is strongest, which is why the
obligation is written into the statement.


5. RIGIDITY OF PHASE DIAGRAMS

Detection thresholds are known to be edge-singular: two nuisance structures can
agree on every weakly continuous functional and have different thresholds. That
is a negative result about summaries. Its positive counterpart is unposed.

THE TARGET. An exact law determining the full phase diagram of a problem family
— all boundaries, with constants, as a function of the aspect ratio and the
nuisance structure — from finitely many invariants of the structure, together
with a proof that the finite list is complete. The known threshold formula is
the codimension-one shadow of this; what is wanted is the whole diagram,
including where boundaries meet, which is where the interesting behaviour and
none of the current theory lives.

IMMUNITY. Proportional-asymptotic phase diagrams are a post-2000 object; the
edge-singularity result that makes the question sharp is this program's.

EVALUATION OBLIGATION. Output the location of one multi-boundary junction with
its constant.


------------------------------------------------------------------
PART V. ORDER, AND ONE STANDING REQUEST
------------------------------------------------------------------

Order of attempt: continue the Design Functor to K1, since it is open and
running and the adversary hunt is cheap. Then target 1, the mechanism
completeness theorem, because the vertex-weight law has just made it
attemptable and because it terminates a cycle that has now run three times.
Targets 2 and 4 next, in either order; they are the two whose success produces
something runnable. Targets 3 and 5 are slower burns and should wait for a
session that is not already carrying an open campaign.

THE STANDING REQUEST, which costs almost nothing and would have saved the last
campaign: at the top of every session log, one paragraph applying the four
immunity tests to that session's target, written before the first theorem. Name
the primitives and their age. Name the instance on which the law must evaluate.
Name the ancestor to be recovered. Name what a refutation would teach. If any
of the four cannot be filled in, that is the finding, and it is worth more than
whatever the session would otherwise have produced.

One last note on what was learned, since it is easy to file this as a loss.
Problem 4 was ranked first by consequence in the brief, and it was ranked first
by me. The ranking was wrong for a reason that is now explicit and checkable,
and the checking procedure costs one paragraph per session. That is a better
outcome than the campaign succeeding would have been, because a success would
have left the selection process unexamined and it would have failed later on
something more expensive.
