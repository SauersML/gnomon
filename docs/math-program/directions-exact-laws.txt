DIRECTIONS: FIVE EXACT LAWS
===========================
A brief to the team, superseding nothing and duplicating nothing.

The previous four lists asked what is impossible, what is classifiable, and what is
approximable. Those were the right questions and the answers have been good. This
brief asks for something categorically different, and the difference is the whole
point of the document.

WE WANT EXACT LAWS.

By an exact law I mean a theorem of the form: given the following inputs, the answer
is THIS — a closed-form map from a described object to a number, a law, or a
procedure, with the constants included and not absorbed into a Theta. Not a rate. Not
a characterization up to universal factors. Not an existence statement with a
non-constructive optimizer. An exact law is a machine: you put the description in and
the answer comes out, and it is right.

The reason for the demand is not aesthetic. Every result of the form "the rate is
Theta(f(n))" leaves the user of the theorem to guess the constant, and in every
application the constant is what decides whether a thing is done. More importantly,
exact laws have a property that rate theorems do not: their proofs are usually
constructions, and the construction is usually a procedure. A sharp variational
formula for a threshold hands you the optimal test as its dual optimizer. A closed
form for a limit hands you the inverse problem — build the object with the limit you
want. This is where methods nobody has conceived of come from: not from trying to
invent a method, but from solving an optimization exactly and reading off what sits
at the optimum. Several times in this program the optimizer has turned out to be an
object with no name in the literature. That should be the expectation, not a
surprise.

Second standing demand, equal in weight to the first: THESE MUST NOT BE
REFORMULATIONS. A problem that resolves to "this is Wu-Yang with different notation"
or "this is the Donoho-Liu modulus in other variables" is worthless here, and the
program has already spent effort discovering such equivalences after the fact. Each
problem below is stated so that a literature search either kills it outright — in
which case kill it early and say so — or confirms it is unposed. The two-search rule
applies before any of these hardens. Where I know of the nearest neighbour I have
named it, and named what makes the problem different; where a problem is a strict
generalization of something known, I say which known thing is the degenerate case,
because a generalization that does not recover its ancestor is usually wrong.

Third: ambition. Calibrate to problems whose solution reorganizes a subject rather
than advancing it. Multi-session incompleteness is expected. A partial exact law on a
restricted class is worth more than a complete rate theorem in general, because the
restricted exact law tells you what the general one looks like and the rate theorem
does not.


------------------------------------------------------------------
NOTATION
------------------------------------------------------------------

A DESIGN of order d is a finite family of subsets of [d] with real coefficients,
normalized to unit second moment; it determines the multilinear form
f = sum_S w_S prod_{i in S} x_i with x_i i.i.d. from a coordinate law nu of mean 0
and variance 1. A design sequence is ADMISSIBLE if minimum degree diverges and
maximal coordinate influence vanishes.

An EXPERIMENT is a family (P_theta) of laws on a standard Borel space indexed by a
standard Borel set. A REDUCTION is a Markov kernel out of the sample space. A
NUISANCE CLASS is a convex weak-* closed set of positive operators.

For a functional phi on an interval, E_K(phi) denotes the degree-K best uniform
approximation error, and the classical constants of that theory (Bernstein's
constant for the absolute value, the Akhiezer-Krein constants for its relatives) are
denoted as usual.


==================================================================
1. THE DESIGN FUNCTOR
   An exact law taking a design to its limit law
==================================================================

Recent work in this program establishes that the closure of the set of limit laws of
admissible chaos is the entire moment body — every centered law with second moment at
most one — and that the limit is not determined by any profile statistics of the
design, since two designs with identical coefficient profiles, identical incidence
column masses at every order, and identical truncated variances can have different
limits. In the quadratic case the invariant that does determine the limit was
identified: the spectrum of the outer coefficient matrix.

That identification is the entire content of what is currently known, and it is a
single case of what should be a general law.

THE LAW SOUGHT. Construct an explicit map

    L : (design data) x (finitely many constants of nu)  ->  (limit law),

exact, with the following properties: it is defined on all admissible design
sequences; it is computable from the design's structure without reference to nu
beyond the finitely many constants; and it is complete, in that two design sequences
have the same limit if and only if their images agree.

Q1a. Identify the design invariant. In the quadratic case it is an eigenvalue
     multiset. Conjecture: in general it is the limit, in a suitable topology, of the
     normalized SHAPE TENSOR of the design — the symmetric tensor of coefficients
     modulo the equivalence generated by variable relabeling and by the operations
     that provably preserve limits. Determine that equivalence exactly. It is
     presumably finer than profile equality and coarser than isomorphism, and pinning
     it is the crux.

Q1b. Identify the constants of nu that survive. Evidence to date: the size-biased
     drift and increment variance (a two-jet of a Mellin transform at a specific
     point), the arithmetic type of the log-square, the symmetry of the law, and —
     newly — the cumulants of the squared coordinate, entering through variables
     shared at multiplicity j. Conjecture: the complete list is the two-jet, the
     arithmetic type, and the full cumulant sequence of the squared coordinate.
     Prove completeness of the list, which is the statement that nothing else about
     nu can be seen at any degree by any design.

Q1c. Exactness. The law must produce the limit itself, not its type. For the
     quadratic case it produces a specific weighted sum of chi-squares with a specific
     characteristic function. State and prove the general analogue.

Q1d. THE INVERSE PROBLEM, which is where the methods are. Given a target law in the
     moment body, construct a design realizing it, and characterize the fiber of L
     over that target. The existence half is known — the moment body is attained — but
     existence was proved by a construction that is almost certainly not the useful
     one. What is wanted is a canonical, minimal, or otherwise distinguished
     representative in each fiber, and an algorithm producing it.

WHY THIS IS NOT A REFORMULATION. The convergence of off-diagonal symmetric statistics
to Hermite functionals is classical, and the maximal-spectrum theorem is a
consequence. What is asked here is the inverse and the classification: not "which
laws arise" but "which design gives which law, exactly, and how do I build the design
for a law I want". No part of the classical theory addresses the fiber structure,
because the classical theory fixes the design and varies the input.

WHY IT IS HARD. Q1a asks for the correct notion of equivalence on an infinite family
of tensors of growing order, where the only currently known invariant is a spectrum
in the one case where the tensor is a matrix. The natural guesses — moment tensors,
graph limits of the incidence hypergraph — are each blind to phenomena the other
sees, and the shape tensor lives in a space with no natural compactness.

WHAT IT GIVES. Q1d is the payoff and it is a genuinely new capability: statistics
with prescribed limit laws, built to order. Every calibration difficulty in this
subject arises because a statistic's null law is whatever the construction happened to
produce; an exact inverse law lets the null be chosen first and the statistic
constructed second. I do not know of any subject in which this has been possible, and
I expect the resulting constructions to be objects no one would have written down.


==================================================================
2. THE CERTIFICATE LAW
   An exact variational formula for detection thresholds
==================================================================

Recent work establishes, for nuisance classes cut out by linear constraints and a
rank-one signal, that the imitation capacity is the value of an explicit linear
program, that rigidity is a normal-cone condition rather than a symmetry condition,
and that on a restricted class — where the binding constraint's load is constant over
the signal prior — the detection threshold EQUALS the program value, two-sidedly, with
the optimal test synthesized as the empirical dual certificate.

The restriction is unsatisfying and the linearity of the constraints is an accident of
convenience. The law should hold in general.

THE LAW SOUGHT. For a convex nuisance class K and a signal family S, an exact formula

    threshold(K, S)  =  value of an explicit variational problem in the support
                        function of K and the geometry of S,

with the optimal test given by the dual optimizer, valid without the equi-load
restriction and for signal families of rank greater than one.

Q2a. Beyond equi-load. When the binding constraint's load varies over the signal
     prior, the scalar formula must fail. Determine what replaces it. Conjecture: a
     finite-dimensional variational problem whose dimension is the number of active
     constraints, so that the threshold is an order-d functional with d the active-set
     size, recovering order one exactly when a single constraint binds. Prove it, and
     give the exact value.

Q2b. Beyond rank one. For signal families of rank r, is the threshold governed by an
     r-dimensional program, and is the optimal test still the empirical dual? The
     naive guess — apply the rank-one law to the worst direction — is provably wrong
     in adjacent settings, so the correct object is presumably a program over the
     Grassmannian.

Q2c. Beyond Gaussian. The formula's derivation uses Gaussianity twice: in identifying
     the mixture and in the concentration of the empirical certificate. Determine
     which of the two is essential. Conjecture: the mixture step is, and the class of
     laws for which the certificate law holds exactly is characterized by an
     injectivity property of a Laplace-type transform on the cone of admissible
     perturbations.

Q2d. Exact constants. Everything above should be stated with the constant, not up to
     one. The known instance has an exact constant; a general theorem with a lost
     constant would be a rate theorem wearing a formula's clothes.

WHY NOT A REFORMULATION. Minimax duality gives the existence of a least-favourable
prior and therefore a saddle value; that is not an exact law, because evaluating the
saddle is as hard as the original problem. The content here is that the saddle is
attained at a point determined by the CONSTRAINT GEOMETRY of the class and is
therefore computable from the class description. The known instance is not a
specialization of any general theorem I can find; it was proved directly.

WHAT IT GIVES. The dual optimizer is the test. Solving Q2a and Q2b produces optimal
procedures for composite detection problems where current practice uses a statistic
chosen by convention. The historical example worth keeping in mind: in the one case
where this law is known, the scalar it produces is not either of the two functionals
the subject had been using for decades, and both of those turned out to be certificates
of nothing.

3. THE PHI-DICTIONARY
   An exact translation between approximation theory and statistical difficulty
==================================================================

A theorem now in hand states that the completeness of a graded lower-bound calculus on
a problem is equivalent to grade-insensitivity of the problem's exchange modulus, and
that for the absolute-value functional the deficit is governed by classical
approximation constants. One instance of a dictionary is proved. The dictionary itself
is conjectural and is worth far more than the instance.

THE LAW SOUGHT. For a functional phi and a model, exact formulas

    minimax risk         =  F( the approximation-theoretic profile of phi ),
    grade-K certified    =  F_K( the same profile ),
    certification gap    =  their ratio,

where the profile is a classical object — the sequence of best-approximation errors,
or a K-functional, or a Peetre-type interpolation invariant — and F, F_K are explicit.

Q3a. Identify the correct profile. Best uniform approximation errors are the obvious
     candidate and are almost certainly not the right one in general, because they are
     blind to the location of the singularity relative to the model's geometry.
     Determine what replaces them.

Q3b. Prove the dictionary in one direction as an exact statement with constants for a
     natural family of nonsmooth functionals broader than the absolute value — the
     positive part, the maximum of finitely many linear functionals, a general convex
     piecewise-linear functional — and determine whether the constants are the
     classical approximation constants or their statistical analogues.

Q3c. The saturation boundary, exactly. Linear functionals saturate at grade two. The
     absolute value never saturates. Determine the exact boundary: characterize the
     functionals whose modulus saturates at grade K, for each finite K. This is a
     complete stratification of functionals by certifiability, and it should be a
     smoothness-type classification.

Q3d. Computational grading. Replace "certificate with K atoms" by "certificate
     evaluable within a stated computational budget" and ask for the same dictionary.
     If it exists, statistical and computational hardness are two evaluations of one
     approximation-theoretic profile, which would be a structural explanation for
     coincidences currently observed case by case.

WHY NOT A REFORMULATION. The known correspondence is one instance, obtained by
computing both sides. A dictionary is a theorem about all functionals, and the only
comparable existing statement — the modulus-of-continuity theory for linear
functionals — is precisely the degenerate case where the profile is trivial.

WHAT IT GIVES. A table. Given a functional, look up its approximation profile and read
off its exact minimax rate and the exact deficit of every standard lower-bound method.
This replaces a research project per functional with a lookup, and Q3d would extend it
to the computational side, where the current state of the art is conjectures that have
recently failed twice.


==================================================================
4. THE ACHIEVABLE POWER BODY
   An exact description of what an experiment can do, and its extreme points
==================================================================

This is the problem I would most like solved, and the one most likely to produce a
procedure nobody has conceived of.

For a simple-versus-simple problem, the set of achievable pairs (level, power) is
described exactly by the Neyman-Pearson lemma, and the extreme points are the
likelihood-ratio tests. That is an exact law, it is over a century old, and it has no
general analogue.

THE LAW SOUGHT. For an experiment (P_theta) and a decision problem, describe EXACTLY
the set of achievable power functions

    B  =  { theta -> E_theta[ delta ]  :  delta a test }  subset of [0,1]^Theta,

as a convex body: its support function, its extreme points, and its faces.

Q4a. The support function. B is convex and its support function against a signed
     measure m on Theta is the total positive part of the m-integral of the densities
     — a quantity that is explicit. So B is, in principle, described. The content is to
     turn that into a usable description: determine the face structure, and determine
     which signed measures expose which faces.

Q4b. THE EXTREME POINTS. For the two-point problem the extreme points are the
     likelihood-ratio tests. For a general experiment they are not known, and they are
     exactly the procedures that cannot be improved in any direction. Characterize them.
     Conjecture: they are the tests whose acceptance region is a sublevel set of a
     finite signed combination of densities, with the combination ranging over a set
     determined by the geometry of B — a Neyman-Pearson lemma for composite problems,
     with an exact description of which combinations arise. Prove it, or find the
     obstruction.

Q4c. Dimension and degeneracy. For which experiments does B have finite-dimensional
     faces, and what is the exact dimension? A finite-dimensional face is a statement
     that a finite family of statistics suffices for all optimal decisions in that
     region — a sufficiency statement with an exact count.

Q4d. The inverse problem. Given a desired power function, decide membership in B and
     construct the test attaining it. Exactness matters here more than anywhere else:
     an approximate membership test is useless because the interesting targets sit on
     the boundary.

WHY NOT A REFORMULATION. The convexity of B and the formula for its support function
are textbook. What is not known, and what is asked, is the extreme-point structure for
composite problems. The literature treats this by imposing structure until a known
lemma applies — monotone likelihood ratio, exponential families, invariance — and each
such assumption is a way of avoiding the general question. The general question has an
exact answer or a provable obstruction, and neither is on record.

WHY IT IS HARD. B lives in a function space over Theta and its extreme points are not
approachable by finite-dimensional intuition; the natural compactness is weak-* and the
natural description is via a duality whose dual side is a space of signed measures with
no useful structure imposed. The one solved case is solved because the dual side is
two-dimensional.

WHAT IT GIVES. Every optimal procedure for every composite decision problem, as the
extreme points of an explicitly described body. If Q4b has a positive answer, the
answer IS a construction, and it will produce tests for problems where current practice
uses a statistic chosen because someone once wrote it down. This is the single clearest
route in this brief to a method nobody would have invented: the extreme points of a
body are found by solving an optimization, not by having an idea, and the solved
two-point case is the only reason likelihood-ratio tests are known at all.

5. THE JOINT REGION
   An exact uncertainty principle for simultaneous objectives
==================================================================

For a single objective, data processing gives an exact monotonicity. For several
objectives at once, nothing exists: per-objective data processing bounds each
coordinate separately and therefore describes only a box, while the truth is a strictly
smaller region whose shape is the entire content.

An exact frontier is known for two objectives under linear reductions in a Gaussian
model, obtained by relaxing to a spectrahedron and observing that both objectives are
linear there. The general problem is open in every direction: more objectives,
nonlinear reductions, non-Gaussian models.

THE LAW SOUGHT. For a finite family of objectives and a class of reductions, the EXACT
achievable region of efficiency vectors, with its extreme points identified as explicit
reductions.

Q5a. THE JOINT DATA-PROCESSING INEQUALITY. Find the correct general statement. The
     shape of the answer should be: risks are concave functionals of a standard
     measure, garbling is a dilation in the convex order, and the achievable region is
     the image of a dilation-ideal under a vector of concave functionals. Make this
     exact and prove it. This is the missing technology and everything else in this
     problem is downstream of it.

Q5b. Three or more objectives. For two, the frontier is a curve and the extreme points
     are coordinate splits. For three, the region is a body and I do not know its
     shape; in particular I do not know whether it is a simplex-like object determined
     by pairwise data or whether genuine three-way interaction appears. Determine
     which, exactly. A genuine three-way term would be the first evidence that
     multi-objective reduction is not reducible to pairwise trade-offs.

Q5c. Beyond linear reductions. Extend the frontier to all measurable reductions in the
     Gaussian model. Conjecture: the linear frontier binds, and the proof requires an
     extremal principle forcing optimizers of the joint program to be linear. Prove or
     refute; a refutation would exhibit a nonlinear reduction beating every linear one
     at two objectives simultaneously, which would itself be a new method.

Q5d. The exact price of sharing. Define the modularity gap as the exact ratio between
     the best single shared reduction and the vector of individually optimal ones, and
     compute it in closed form for the Gaussian model. This is a number people
     currently do not know they are paying.

WHY NOT A REFORMULATION. Multi-objective statistical decision theory exists but is
concerned with admissibility and complete classes, not with the exact geometry of an
achievable efficiency region under a bottleneck. The information-bottleneck literature
treats one objective with one constraint. The joint region for several objectives with
a shared reduction appears to be unposed.

WHAT IT GIVES. Q5b is the most interesting scientifically: whether multi-objective
trade-offs are pairwise or genuinely higher-order is a structural fact about
information that nobody has determined. Q5c's refutation branch would produce a
nonlinear reduction with no current analogue. And Q5d turns an architectural decision
made by taste into a computed number.


------------------------------------------------------------------
HOW TO CHOOSE, AND WHAT COUNTS AS PROGRESS
------------------------------------------------------------------

Ranked by expected consequence if solved: 4, 1, 3, 2, 5. Problem 4 first because its
positive branch is a construction that outputs optimal procedures for problems where
none are known, and because the one solved case of it is the foundation of an entire
subject. Problem 1 second because its inverse half is a capability rather than a
theorem.

Ranked by expected difficulty, hardest first: 4b, 1a, 3a, 5c, 2b. Note that the two
rankings agree at the top, which is usually a sign that the problem is correctly
chosen rather than a sign that it is hopeless.

What counts as progress, in decreasing order of value:
  - an exact law on a restricted class, with constants;
  - a proof that no exact law exists in a stated class, with the obstruction named;
  - a rate theorem, only if it is accompanied by the reason the constant is
    inaccessible;
  - a reduction of one of these problems to a named open problem elsewhere, which is
    worth reporting immediately rather than at the end, since it changes what should
    be attempted next.

Two standing instructions. Run the literature searches EARLY, not before publication:
the cost of discovering after three sessions that a problem is a known theorem in other
notation has already been paid once in this program and should not be paid again. And
attack the definitions adversarially before attacking the theorems — twice in the
recent campaign an attempt to break one's own definition produced the correct
definition, and in both cases the corrected definition was what made the subsequent
proof short.
