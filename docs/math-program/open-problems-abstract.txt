OPEN PROBLEMS
=============

Eight problems. Items that are routine computations, or that are already
settled in the literature and merely need to be located and applied, have
been excluded; the exclusions are listed at the end so they are not
reintroduced.

Difficulty: [RESEARCH] = open in its own right; [HARD] = serious project,
path visible.


------------------------------------------------------------------
0. STANDING NOTATION
------------------------------------------------------------------

Let P and Q be probability measures on R^d x R, written (X, Y), with
finite second moments and X centered under each. Write

    A = Cov_P(X),  a = Cov_P(X, Y),  u = A^{-1} a,
    B = Cov_Q(X),  b = Cov_Q(X, Y),  v = B^{-1} b,

so u and v are the L^2-optimal linear coefficients under P and Q. For a
vector w put R_M(w) = E_M (Y - <w, X>)^2 for M in {P, Q}. Whenever B is
invertible,

    R_Q(w) - R_Q(v) = (w - v)^T B (w - v).                          (*)

The recurring situation is that w is selected using P and evaluated under
Q. Call A -> B the *second-moment shift* and u -> v the *coefficient
shift*.


------------------------------------------------------------------
TIER 1
------------------------------------------------------------------

1. DETECTION LIMIT FOR A SPIKE IN DEPENDENT-COLUMN NOISE      [RESEARCH]

   Let Z in R^{n x m} have i.i.d. mean-zero, unit-variance entries, let
   K in R^{m x m} be positive semidefinite, and set E = Z K^{1/2}, so E
   has independent rows and column covariance K. Consider

       W = (1 / tr K) (theta p q^T + E)(theta p q^T + E)^T,

   with p in S^{n-1}, q in S^{m-1}. Normalizing by tr K gives
   E[E E^T / tr K] = I_n, reducing to 1/m when K = I. Take n, m ->
   infinity with n/m -> gamma in (0, infinity) and the spectral measure
   of K convergent.

   Q1a. Below the spectral threshold theta_c, is the spike undetectable
        by *every* test with vanishing total error, or only by spectral
        methods? For i.i.d. Gaussian columns the spiked and null laws are
        mutually contiguous below threshold and no test succeeds.
        Whether contiguity survives general K is open, and the answer
        cannot be read off from the eigenvalue analysis.

   Q1b. Above threshold, bound the overlap |<p, p_hat>| between the true
        spike direction and the top eigenvector of W, *uniformly* over K
        with a prescribed limiting spectrum. Pointwise-in-K results do
        not suffice for any downstream claim quantified over K.

   Remark. The location of theta_c itself is not posed as open: the
   generalized spiked-model theory for noise with a general population
   covariance already supplies the machinery, and applying it will show
   that theta_c depends on more than the participation ratio
   m_eff = (tr K)^2 / tr(K^2). That computation is worth doing, but it
   is derivation, not discovery. The genuinely open content is Q1a and
   the uniformity in Q1b.

   Q1a is the difference between "this method fails" and "the problem is
   impossible." Only the latter licenses an impossibility claim, and the
   same distinction recurs in Problem 8.


2. IDENTIFYING THE DIRECTION OF CONDITIONAL EXCESS RISK           [HARD]

   Fix w and let delta = w - v. Under Q, with m_Q(x) = E_Q[Y | X = x],

       E_Q[(Y - <w, X>)^2 | X = x] - E_Q[(Y - <v, X>)^2 | X = x]
           = (m_Q(x) - <w, x>)^2 - (m_Q(x) - <v, x>)^2,

   which when m_Q is linear collapses to the rank-one quadratic form

       r(x) = <delta, x>^2.                                        (**)

   Conditional excess risk is thus a squared linear functional along one
   unknown direction, and the conditional variance of Y cancels
   identically. Evaluating r is trivial; identifying delta is not, since
   delta depends on v, which is not estimable from P alone.

   Q2a. Which observable summaries of (P, Q) determine delta, or the
        projections of delta that dominate the law of r(X) under Q?

   Q2b. Call T: R^d -> R^k with r(x) = f(T(x)) on supp(Q) a *summary of
        order k*. Prove a lower bound on the least admissible k over a
        natural class of shifts (A, B, u, v). In particular show that no
        scalar summary of the P-to-Q discrepancy determines the law of
        r(X), and exhibit the obstruction.

   Q2c. When m_Q is nonlinear, (**) acquires a misspecification term.
        Separate the two contributions; this connects to Problem 7.

   The negative half of Q2b is easy — two configurations can share any
   given scalar discrepancy while inducing different laws for r(X). The
   positive half, the smallest sufficient statistic, is open, and is the
   only item here whose solution is immediately an estimator.


3. MINIMAX RISK OVER THE REACHABLE SET OF A MARKOV FLOW       [RESEARCH]

   Let (B_t, v_t) be a Markov process on PSD(d) x R^d with generator L.
   Run two independent copies from a common initial state for time t,
   obtaining P_t and Q_t; let C_t be the set of reachable pairs. Given
   n_P samples from P_t and n_Q from Q_t, define

       Rate(n_P, n_Q, t)
           = inf_{w_hat} sup_{(P, Q) in C_t} E[ R_Q(w_hat) - R_Q(v) ].

   Q3a. Determine Rate up to constants, with its t-dependence expressed
        through invariants of L rather than an abstract distance between
        P and Q.

   Q3b. Exhibit an estimator attaining it, and locate the critical ratio
        n_Q / n_P at which the target sample ceases to bind.

   Standard minimax transfer-learning theory takes the shift class to be
   a divergence ball. Here it is the reachable set of a specified
   dynamics: smaller, anisotropic, curved. The rate should reflect the
   geometry of L, and that is what separates this from a specialization
   of known results.


4. SAMPLE COMPLEXITY OF POST-HOC CORRECTION                       [HARD]

   Let g: R^d -> R be fixed, nu_Q the law of g(X) under Q, and call g
   eps-calibrated under Q if

       || E_Q[Y | g(X) = s] - s ||_{L^2(nu_Q)} <= eps.

   Consider F_1 = { s -> c_0 + c_1 s } and
   F_2 = { s -> phi(s) : phi nondecreasing }.

   Q4a. For each family determine n(eps, delta): samples from Q needed to
        select a correction that is eps-calibrated with probability
        1 - delta, with matching upper and lower bounds in the shift
        magnitude. The lower bound is the substance; the upper bound
        follows from standard uniform-convergence arguments.

   Q4b. Does F_2 strictly enlarge the correctable class beyond F_1, or
        only improve constants? Every element of F_2 is a function of
        g(X) alone, so the obstruction to correcting a second-moment
        shift — that no map of <u, x> reproduces <v, x> pointwise unless
        v is proportional to u — appears to persist into the monotone
        family. A proof would sharply delimit what post-hoc correction
        can do, and a counterexample would be more interesting still.

TIER 2
------------------------------------------------------------------

5. COVARIANCE KERNEL OF A PARTITION-VALUED PROCESS                [HARD]

   Let (Pi_t) be a Markov process on partitions of {1, ..., N} specified
   by two mechanisms: any two blocks merge at a fixed rate, and each
   index leaves its block to start a new one at a rate c(i, j) depending
   on its position relative to the others. Put

       k_t(i, j) = P( i and j lie in the same block of Pi_t ),

   with induced operator K_t.

   Q5a. Characterize K_t and its spectrum. Only the two-index marginal
        is currently available, and only in a discrete-time
        approximation.

   Q5b. Determine the decay of k_t(i, j) in |i - j| and its dependence
        on the rate function c.

   Q5c. Give the asymptotics of ||K_t - K_{t'}|| for t < t', in operator
        and Frobenius norm. Only a lower bound via an explicit two-index
        witness is available. This quantity controls the size of the
        shift in Problem 3, so a rate here feeds directly into that
        minimax problem.


6. REGULARIZED REGRESSION UNDER SECOND-MOMENT SHIFT           [RESEARCH]

   Identity (*) presupposes B invertible. When d >= n the sample second
   moment is singular and the decomposition is unavailable. Let w_lambda
   be the ridge estimator fit on n samples from P.

   Q6a. In the proportional regime d/n -> gamma in (0, infinity), with A
        and B general — non-identity and non-commuting — determine the
        limit of R_Q(w_lambda) in terms of gamma, lambda, and the joint
        spectral behavior of (A, B). The commuting case is understood;
        non-commutativity is the obstruction, since A and B are then not
        simultaneously diagonalizable and bias and variance do not
        decouple coordinatewise.

   Q6b. Determine the optimal lambda, and whether risk is monotone in
        gamma or peaks near gamma = 1 as in the matched case A = B.

   Q6c. Give the analogue of (*) valid for ridge and for the
        pseudo-inverse, so downstream results need not assume
        invertibility.

TIER 3
------------------------------------------------------------------

7. MOVEMENT OF A PROJECTION UNDER CHANGE OF MEASURE               [HARD]

   Suppose E_M[Y | X] = h(X) with h fixed and nonlinear, and let u and v
   be the L^2(P)- and L^2(Q)-projections of h onto span{X_1, ..., X_d}.

   Q7a. Bound ||u - v|| in terms of the nonlinearity of h and the
        discrepancy between P and Q. The projection moves under change of
        measure even though h is unchanged, so an observed coefficient
        shift need not indicate any change in the underlying
        relationship.

   Q7b. Decompose the excess risk (*) into a genuine-shift term and a
        projection-artifact term, and determine which dominates as a
        function of the curvature of h.

   A crude bound in Q7a is routine; what is wanted is a sharp
   decomposition in Q7b, since the practical question is whether a
   measured shift is real, and that requires the two terms separated
   rather than jointly bounded.


8. MINIMAX WEIGHTING ACROSS HETEROGENEOUS BLOCKS                  [HARD]

   Let a common signal be observed through blocks c = 1, ..., C, block c
   supplying d_c coordinates at per-coordinate signal strength s_c.
   Within the family of linear block-weightings, weights proportional to
   d_c s_c are optimal.

   Q8. Is that weighting minimax optimal over *all* estimators given
       (d_c, s_c)? Prove or refute a matching lower bound. As in Q1a, the
       question is whether the failure of a particular procedure is the
       failure of the problem; optimality within a family is not
       evidence of optimality outside it.


------------------------------------------------------------------
EXCLUDED, AND WHY
------------------------------------------------------------------

The following were considered and dropped. They are recorded so they are
not mistaken for gaps.

- Endpoint correlation of a mean-reverting diffusion. Two copies of an
  Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process sharing a path and then evolving
  independently are jointly Gaussian, and the correlation has a closed
  form obtained by propagating the covariance through the linear
  dynamics. This is a computation, not a problem, and it means the
  correlation parameter is available on demand rather than being a
  standing barrier.

- Berry-Esseen constants and Edgeworth expansions for weighted sums of
  independent bounded lattice variables. Explicit constants,
  non-uniform bounds, and the lattice correction term are all classical.
  Obtaining them is literature retrieval.

- Equality conditions in a two-form Cauchy-Schwarz bound. Equality in
  Cauchy-Schwarz is proportionality; the induced gap is a direct
  computation once the two Gram matrices are written out.

- Moments of a normal variable conditioned on exceeding a symmetric
  threshold, composed with a correlated perturbation. Truncated normal
  moments are classical via the Mills ratio, and the composition is a
  product of two known factors.

- Pareto frontier for simultaneous calibration and error-rate parity
  across subpopulations with unequal signal-to-noise. The qualitative
  incompatibility is known and the frontier is a constrained
  optimization over a two-dimensional feasible set. Peripheral to the
  estimation questions above.


------------------------------------------------------------------
STRUCTURAL REMARK
------------------------------------------------------------------

Problems 1, 3, and 6 are recognizable open problems in random matrix
theory and high-dimensional statistics. Problems 2 and 4 are the ones
whose solutions are directly estimators.

The recurring weakness is that the dynamical layer — a stochastic process
generating the discrepancy, Problem 5 — and the decision layer — risk and
calibration under that discrepancy, Problems 2, 3, 4 — are joined by
quantities currently supplied as hypotheses rather than derived: the
operator gap ||K_t - K_{t'}|| and the effective dimension summarizing a
dependent noise covariance. Problems 5 and 1 are exactly the tasks of
deriving those two from the dynamics. (The third such quantity, the
correlation between two branches of the flow, turned out to be a closed-
form computation; see the exclusions.) Resolving either converts a large
family of conditional statements into unconditional ones.
